A record request can arrive by portal, mail, fax, email, in person, or through another organization. Custody means the practice can trace the request from receipt through identity and authority review, scope clarification, fulfillment, delivery, and final evidence. It does not mean clerical staff decide what may be disclosed.

## Quick overview:

- Date-stamp the original request and assign one request ID.
- Keep requester identity, asserted authority, requested scope, and delivery preference as separate fields.
- Route disclosure, denial, amendment, legal process, and sensitive-record questions to authorized reviewers.
- Record preparation and delivery as different stages.
- Close with evidence of the approved disposition and delivery result.

## Preserve the request as received

Store the source in the approved repository without rewriting it. Capture request ID, receipt channel, received date and time, requester name, relationship claimed, person or records named, date range, requested format, delivery destination, and any deadline asserted by the requester. An asserted deadline is not automatically the governing deadline, so retain it as source information for authorized review.

If the same request arrives twice, link the sources to one custody record only after confirming they are duplicates. Do not erase either receipt timestamp. A follow-up letter may change scope and should be preserved as a new event rather than treated as a duplicate.

HHS provides first-party guidance on the [right of access](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/access/index.html). Practices should use qualified guidance to determine which rules apply to a particular request, including state law and special record categories.

## Separate checks that are often conflated

Identity verification asks whether the requester is who they claim to be. Authority review asks whether that requester may act or receive information in the asserted capacity. Scope review determines which records and date ranges are covered. Availability identifies where responsive material resides. Each can have a different owner and result.

Use explicit states such as identity evidence pending, authority review pending, scope clarification requested, records search in progress, authorized for preparation, exception review, ready for approved delivery, delivered, delivery failed, and closed by authorized disposition. "Processing" hides too much.

Administrative staff may perform approved identity steps, index documents, run authorized searches, assemble candidate records, and record delivery evidence. They should not interpret a subpoena, determine a representative's legal authority, decide an exception, select redactions, or make clinical judgments about content.

## Build a chain of custody

Every transfer should answer who had the request or prepared package, when custody changed, what artifact moved, and where it is stored. The log can include:

| Event | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| receipt | source artifact and timestamp |
| verification request | approved message and destination |
| reviewer assignment | named role and assigned time |
| scope decision | authorized instruction reference |
| preparation | preparer, record set reference, page or file count where used |
| quality check | checker and approved comparison result |
| delivery | method, destination, timestamp, tracking or system result |
| failure or return | error evidence and next owner |

Avoid copying record content into the custody log. The log should point to the controlled source and package.

## Treat delivery as a controlled event

Before release, compare the approved recipient and destination with the delivery transaction. A requester's original email address may not be an approved destination. Use the practice's authorized channels and verification steps.

Suppose a portal request asks for records to be sent to an unfamiliar third-party email, while a later phone call asks for paper pickup. Preserve both instructions. Do not choose the more convenient option. Route the destination conflict to the person authorized to confirm or decide it, then record the supported instruction.

After sending, capture whether the transmission succeeded, failed, was returned, or remains unconfirmed. "Prepared" and "sent" are not interchangeable with "delivered." If a package is returned, reopen the delivery task while preserving the initial attempt.

## Monitor deadlines without making legal conclusions

The queue can calculate review dates from a rule maintained by the practice, but the rule owner determines applicability. Show the basis, start date, paused periods if the approved procedure permits them, and current due date. Flag requests approaching the review threshold and assign them; do not silently recalculate after an amendment.

Quality review can compare receipt sources to the custody log, check that authority decisions have named evidence, sample package-to-scope matching, and inspect delivery failures. Report excluded or unresolved cases rather than presenting a low count as guaranteed compliance.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an administrator decide that a request is valid?

An administrator can complete specifically approved verification steps and document results. Legal authority, disclosure scope, exceptions, and denial decisions remain with the practice's authorized reviewer.

### Should duplicate requests get duplicate packages?

Not automatically. Link suspected duplicates, preserve each source, and have the workflow determine whether the scope or destination differs before fulfillment.

### What proves that a request is complete?

The record needs an authorized disposition, evidence that preparation matched that disposition, and the delivery or other closure result required by the procedure. A generated file alone is not completion.

## Keep every handoff attributable

Request custody succeeds when staff can reconstruct the source, authorized decision, prepared package, and delivery outcome without exposing content in the tracker. Coordinate account controls through [EHR access offboarding](/blog/mental-health-ehr-access-offboarding) and govern delegated identities through [portal proxy access controls](/blog/control-behavioral-health-portal-proxy-access). A precise chain protects continuity while keeping disclosure judgment with the appropriate role.
