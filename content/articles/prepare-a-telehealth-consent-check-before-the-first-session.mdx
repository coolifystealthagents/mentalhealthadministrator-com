## How to approach: Prepare a telehealth consent check before the first session

A telehealth consent check should confirm that the client understands the platform and the limits before the session starts. Start with the client's identity, the platform, the location, and the emergency plan. A consent form that is signed but not explained is not informed consent. Keep the original note beside the corrected version. The authorization should be checked before the session. The review should end with one owner and one date.

Keep the check to the few facts the clinician needs. A long technical explanation may overwhelm the client. The check should be a conversation, not a download. ## Confirm the client and the platform

Verify the client's identity through the approved method. Confirm the platform and the link before the session. A client who cannot connect at the appointment time may miss the session. Record the client's location and the local emergency number. A clinician needs to know where the client is in an emergency. The location should be current. ## Explain the limits and the plan

Explain the privacy limits of the platform and the practice. Do not promise more security than the platform provides. The client should know what to do if the connection fails. Write the emergency plan with the client. Name the local resource, the person to contact, and the backup phone number. The plan should be in the record. ## Record the consent
