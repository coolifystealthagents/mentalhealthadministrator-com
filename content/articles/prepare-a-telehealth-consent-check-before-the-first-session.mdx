## How to approach: Prepare a telehealth consent check before the first session

A telehealth consent check should confirm that the client understands the platform and the limits before the session starts. Start with the client's identity, the platform, the location, and the emergency plan. A consent form that is signed but not explained is not informed consent. Keep the original note beside the corrected version.

Keep the check to the few facts the clinician needs. A long technical explanation may overwhelm the client. The check should be a conversation, not a download. Write the deadline as a date and a local time.

## Confirm the client and the platform

Verify the client's identity through the approved method. Confirm the platform and the link before the session. A client who cannot connect at the appointment time may miss the session. A short note with four fields is easier to use than a long narrative.

Record the client's location and the local emergency number. A clinician needs to know where the client is in an emergency. The location should be current. A control that depends on memory is not yet a control.

## Explain the limits and the plan

Explain the privacy limits of the platform and the practice. Do not promise more security than the platform provides. The client should know what to do if the connection fails. Use the same format for every handover so the next reader can scan it.

Write the emergency plan with the client. Name the local resource, the person to contact, and the backup phone number. The plan should be in the record. Ask whether a new reviewer could act on the record without a conversation.

## Record the consent

Record the date, the version of the consent, and the person who explained it. A consent without a version is hard to review later. The client should receive a copy. Record the exception and the person who approved it.

If the client declines, record the decision and the alternative. Do not pressure the client. The record should show that the choice was informed. Keep the boundary visible in the place where the work is assigned.

## Review the check quarterly

Review the consent and the emergency plan with the clinical team. A platform change or a new state rule may require an update. Record the review date. Record the exception and the person who approved it.

Keep the old consent with the change note. A later audit may need to see what the client agreed to. The goal is an informed client, not a signed form. A short note with four fields is easier to use than a long narrative.
