## Set the after-hours messages scope

Set an After-Hours Message Boundary starts with a written boundary. Name the client, the source, the owner, and the evidence. A clear rule keeps routine coordination separate from clinical judgment.

The main risk is that a clinical message can wait without an urgent routing rule. Record the source and time before changing a status.

## Capture the evidence

A usable record includes client, message, time, urgency, action, owner, and next step. Keep the source beside the record so another reviewer can reconstruct what happened.

The clinical lead confirms the decision or exception. The assistant can log the message, route urgent issues, and prepare follow-up, but should not fill a gap with an assumption.

## Separate preparation from judgment

Decisions about clinical triage and urgent decisions remain with the authorized clinical or administrative owner. Use three labels: proceed, pause for missing evidence, and escalate.

## Run the review

Review callbacks, urgent routes, and open items. For example, a client reports a crisis after hours. Record what evidence was available and who owned the next step.

## Pilot one workflow

Start with one message channel. Test the checklist on a small batch and review exceptions with the owner.
