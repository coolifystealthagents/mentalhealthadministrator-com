A location confirmation is a time-specific administrative fact, not a permanent attribute of a client. A person may join one visit from home, another from work, and a third while traveling. A useful record therefore shows what location was reported for the particular session, when it was reported, and what happened when that fact changed or could not be confirmed.

## Quick overview:

Build the location check around the practice's approved telehealth procedure. Give staff a neutral prompt, a dedicated field, a timestamp, and a route for exceptions. Keep state-specific practice questions, emergency planning, clinical suitability, and decisions about whether a visit may proceed with authorized personnel. The administrative record should demonstrate collection and routing without pretending that a captured location resolves those questions.

## Treat location as an event, not a profile field

A demographic address may be useful for identity or correspondence, but it does not establish where a person is sitting at appointment time. Avoid silently copying a home address into the session-location field. The better design creates one location event per scheduled encounter and connects it to the appointment identifier.

Useful fields include:

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Appointment ID | Prevents evidence from attaching to the wrong encounter |
| Reported locality and state | Captures the approved level of detail |
| Source channel | Distinguishes portal form, phone response, or live confirmation |
| Captured at and by | Establishes timing and custody |
| Confirmation state | Uses defined values such as confirmed, changed, unavailable, or conflict |
| Exception owner | Identifies who must review a nonroutine condition |
| Prior value reference | Preserves history when a correction occurs |

The practice should decide whether a full street address is required for its workflow. Administrative staff should not collect more precision by habit than the approved procedure calls for.

## Place checks at deliberate points

One early request can detect travel or technology questions before the visit. A second, near-session check can capture a last-minute change. The current value should not overwrite the earlier response. Instead, the record should show a sequence: reminder response received, same-day value confirmed or changed, and exception acknowledged.

For example, a portal form records "Ohio" the day before an appointment. Ten minutes before the visit, the client reports being in Pennsylvania. The coordinator marks the encounter as location conflict, preserves both timestamps, and alerts the designated clinician or operational reviewer. The coordinator does not determine licensure rules, assess whether the session can continue, or tell the client that care is approved.

## Define message and system behavior

Use plain language that asks only for the approved administrative fact. Do not put a sensitive location in an email subject or broadly visible calendar note. If a client replies with symptoms, a safety concern, or a request for clinical guidance, stop the location script and use the practice's established clinical or urgent-message route.

Technology deserves its own controls. A form submission should write to the intended encounter, not merely to the client profile. Required-field logic should not turn "unknown" into an invented value. If an interface imports an old address, label it as demographic data rather than confirmed session location. During an outage, a restricted temporary log needs the appointment reference, minimum location value, capture time, staff identity, and reconciliation state. Move that evidence into the approved system when service returns and close the temporary entry only after comparison.

## Make contradictions visible

A location can conflict with a reminder response, a live statement, or another record. Do not ask staff to select the most plausible source. Configure a conflict state that keeps all reported values and assigns the next decision. Other stop conditions include an unmatched identity, no response by the practice's cutoff, an ambiguous state or locality, a request to conceal the location, or an unavailable authorized owner.

The handoff should answer four questions: Which appointment is affected? What exact administrative values disagree? When and through which channels were they received? Who now owns review? A sent message is evidence of an attempt, not evidence that the reviewer acted.

## Audit the evidence chain

Review a sample by starting with completed appointments and tracing backward. Check whether each eligible encounter has a location event, whether changes retain the prior value, whether conflicts have an acknowledgment, and whether staff stayed within the approved prompt. Also begin with the exception queue and trace forward, since appointment-only sampling can omit unresolved records.

Metrics should describe the process rather than imply clinical quality. Examples are the share of eligible encounters with a timestamped confirmation, number of location changes after the first response, unacknowledged conflicts at session time, and temporary records awaiting reconciliation. Document the denominator, exclusions, extraction time, and definition of acknowledgment before comparing periods.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the address already in the record enough?

Not necessarily. A stored address and a location reported for a particular session are different facts. Follow the practice's approved procedure and do not infer one from the other.

### Can administrative staff decide whether the visit proceeds?

Only if the practice has authorized a purely administrative action for the exact condition. Questions involving professional licensure, clinical appropriateness, safety, or legal interpretation belong with the designated qualified reviewer.

### What if the client changes location after confirming?

Retain the first response, add the new value and time, set the defined conflict or changed status, and route it. Do not erase the history or represent the new value as reviewed before acknowledgment occurs.

### Should staff verify location through an IP address?

Do not introduce technical surveillance as an informal workaround. Use only methods explicitly approved by the practice after appropriate privacy, security, legal, and operational review.

## Close with evidence, not assumption

A location-control record is complete when the required fact has a source and timestamp, any contradiction has reached its named owner, and the resulting status is recorded. It does not establish that the session was clinically appropriate or legally permissible. For broader federal context, review the [HHS telehealth privacy and security guidance](https://telehealth.hhs.gov/providers/telehealth-policy/protecting-patient-privacy).
