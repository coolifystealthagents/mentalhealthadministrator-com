Disabling the main EHR login is only one part of offboarding. A departing worker may also have mobile sessions, portal administration, e-prescribing or billing integrations, report subscriptions, shared mailbox access, API tokens, remote desktop access, and open work assigned to a personal queue. Verifiable closure connects the departure event to each account and each unfinished responsibility.

## Quick overview:

- Start from an authoritative workforce event with an effective cutoff time.
- Inventory direct, federated, vendor, device, and delegated access.
- Reassign queues before or alongside account disablement so work does not become invisible.
- Preserve disablement confirmations and unresolved exceptions.
- Have a second owner verify high-risk or failed removals.

## Translate the departure into a timed control

The offboarding ticket should state the person's unique workforce identifier, role, manager, departure type, last authorized access time with time zone, systems in scope, device obligations, and coordinating owner. Avoid relying on names alone, especially when names are shared or accounts use legacy identifiers.

Different departures can require different timing. The authorized workforce, security, and practice leaders should set the cutoff. An administrator executes the approved schedule but should not independently decide whether suspected misconduct warrants immediate access removal or evidence preservation.

HHS discusses access controls and workforce security in its [Security Rule guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/guidance/index.html). A practice still needs advice suited to its systems, contracts, and applicable obligations. A completed checklist should not be described as a compliance guarantee.

## Build an access map before disablement

Use onboarding records, identity-provider groups, EHR roles, vendor rosters, and device management data to create the scope. Ask about access inherited through groups as well as named accounts. Include:

| Access surface | Closure evidence |
| --- | --- |
| EHR user and privileged roles | disabled timestamp and administrator |
| active web and mobile sessions | revocation result |
| single sign-on groups | membership removal record |
| payer, laboratory, telehealth, and billing portals | vendor confirmation or local removal |
| shared inboxes and calendars | delegation removal |
| exports and scheduled reports | subscription or job cancellation |
| managed laptop, phone, token, or key | return, wipe, or exception record |

Shared credentials defeat attribution and make clean offboarding difficult. Replace them with individual access wherever the system supports it. If a vendor account cannot be removed promptly, document compensating action and escalate rather than marking the row complete.

## Protect continuity of work

Before the account disappears, inventory the user's uncompleted tasks: unsigned or protected clinical work for qualified owners, message baskets, scheduling callbacks, authorization follow-ups, claim inquiries, document exceptions, and record requests. Reassignment must respect role boundaries. A manager should decide the proper recipient for protected work.

Capture the queue name, item count or report reference, transfer recipient, transfer time, and confirmation. Do not bulk assign everything to a generic pool without an accountable owner. Also change automation that routes new work to the departing person, such as referral rules and round-robin schedules.

## Verify the cutoff from multiple views

The system administrator records successful disablement, but the closure reviewer should also verify that authentication fails or the identity provider shows the account disabled, active sessions are revoked where supported, privileged group membership is removed, and connected applications no longer authorize access. Do not ask the former worker to test a login.

Consider a remote biller whose EHR account is disabled at 5 p.m. but whose payer portal login is independent and whose daily export still goes to a managed mailbox. The ticket remains open until those separate surfaces are removed or have approved exceptions. Device recovery is tracked independently so a courier delay does not obscure whether logical access ended.

## Handle exceptions without false closure

Useful exception codes include vendor unreachable, account not found, shared ownership unresolved, device pending return, legal hold review, integration owner unknown, and queue reassignment rejected. Each exception needs an owner and follow-up time. "IT notified" is evidence of a request, not proof of removal.

After closure, compare the offboarding roster against active user lists. Investigate unmatched active accounts, accounts lacking a workforce owner, and recently departed users with post-cutoff activity. The review should preserve evidence and route concerns to the authorized security or privacy role. Administrative reviewers should not investigate content beyond their permission.

Track the number of departures in scope, completed control rows, late confirmations, unknown accounts, and open exceptions. State the reporting period and definitions. These counts test the process; they do not prove that no unauthorized access occurred.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should the EHR account be deleted?

Usually the control objective is to prevent access while preserving attribution and records. Whether to disable, archive, or delete depends on system behavior and approved retention and security policy.

### What happens to the departing person's open inbox?

Inventory and reassign items to authorized owners, confirm the transfer, and update future routing. Account closure without queue custody can hide unfinished work.

### Is device return the same as access termination?

No. Logical access and physical asset recovery are related but separate controls. Record each result and escalate either one if incomplete.

## Close identities, sessions, and ownership

Offboarding is complete when evidence supports the cutoff across the relevant access map and unfinished work has a current owner. Use [portal proxy access controls](/blog/control-behavioral-health-portal-proxy-access) to address delegated identities and [credential expiration controls](/blog/behavioral-health-credential-expiration-controls) where role eligibility changes before departure. The final record should make exceptions visible rather than turning a submitted ticket into assumed closure.
