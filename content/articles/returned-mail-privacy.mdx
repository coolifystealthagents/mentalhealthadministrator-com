## Set the returned mail scope

Handle Returned Mail Without Exposing Details starts with a written boundary. Name the client, the source, the owner, and the evidence. A clear rule keeps routine coordination separate from clinical judgment.

The main risk is that a returned letter can expose sensitive information or be discarded. Record the source and time before changing a status.

## Capture the evidence

A usable record includes client, mail, return reason, date, action, owner, and status. Keep the source beside the record so another reviewer can reconstruct what happened.

The office manager confirms the decision or exception. The assistant can record the return, secure the mail, and route contact questions, but should not fill a gap with an assumption.

## Separate preparation from judgment

Decisions about privacy and clinical decisions remain with the authorized clinical or administrative owner. Use three labels: proceed, pause for missing evidence, and escalate.

## Run the review

Review returned mail, wrong addresses, and documentation. For example, a letter is returned with a forwarding address. Record what evidence was available and who owned the next step.

## Pilot one workflow

Start with one mail type. Test the checklist on a small batch and review exceptions with the owner.
