An external referral is not ready merely because a destination fax number exists. The practice needs to know what was approved for disclosure, which recipient was named, whether the approval is current for this handoff, and what was actually transmitted. Consent review is a controlled checkpoint, not a box for a coordinator to interpret independently.

## Quick overview:

Use a pre-send record that joins the referral order or request, destination identity, approved disclosure basis, document manifest, and authorized release decision. Administrative staff can compare those defined fields and stop mismatches. They should not decide whether broad wording legally permits a disclosure, select clinical material, or expand the recipient. After sending, preserve transport evidence and recipient acknowledgment as separate events.

## Assemble a release packet before transmission

Give the packet its own identifier. At minimum, record the client and referral identifiers, destination organization and intended recipient, verified contact source, consent or other approved authority reference, effective and expiration dates when applicable, authorized information categories, selected document manifest, approving owner, transport channel, and planned send date.

The manifest should list the document title, service date or date range, version, and page count where useful. "Records attached" is too vague to reconstruct a handoff. The packet does not need duplicate copies of every document if secure references are available, but those references must remain stable for the required review period.

## Compare four dimensions

Before release, compare **person**, **purpose**, **destination**, and **content**. A match on three does not cure a conflict in the fourth.

Consider a signed form naming Clinic A while the current referral order identifies Clinic B. The coordinator should not assume the organizations share records, edit the destination, or send to both. Set a destination mismatch, preserve the two source records, and route them to the designated privacy or disclosure reviewer. The reviewer records the authorized disposition; the coordinator then acts on that disposition.

Another mismatch occurs when a consent refers to an evaluation summary but the export contains progress notes and billing pages. Stop the packet. The administrative task is to identify that the manifest exceeds the named category, not to decide which clinical sentences are relevant.

## Verify destination through a trusted source

Do not rely solely on contact information pasted into an email or carried forward from an old referral. Use the practice's approved directory, a verified recipient response, or another defined source. Record where and when the destination was verified. If a fax number or secure endpoint changes, retain the old value and the evidence supporting the correction.

Destination verification and disclosure authorization are separate. A verified fax number does not authorize a release, and a valid release does not prove that an unverified number belongs to the intended recipient.

## Use meaningful packet states

A referral queue should reveal why transmission has not occurred. Useful states include destination verification pending, authority review needed, consent current, manifest mismatch, approved to send, transmission failed, sent awaiting acknowledgment, recipient rejected, and handoff closed.

Define closure explicitly. A successful fax result or secure-message status supports transport, but it may not prove that the intended organization accepted responsibility. If the practice requires acknowledgment, track the recipient, time, channel, and result. If acknowledgment is not required, do not imply one occurred.

## Keep decisions with the right roles

A coordinator can collect existing forms, compare exact names and dates, build a manifest from an approved instruction, use an approved channel, and log attempts. Questions about disclosure authority, revocation, guardianship, sensitive record categories, minimum necessary determinations, or a client's ambiguous instructions require the practice's authorized reviewer and applicable guidance.

Clinical selection is also outside routine routing. A request to "send what they need" should not lead an administrator to choose the most persuasive notes. Require a specific approved document instruction. If the recipient asks for more after receipt, treat that request as a new review event rather than silently appending files.

## Plan for failures after send

A busy signal, invalid secure address, wrong-recipient report, partial page count, or expired download link needs a distinct response. Preserve the original attempt and its result. Do not repeatedly resend to an uncertain endpoint. For a suspected misdirection, stop routine handling and invoke the practice's privacy and incident procedure without making independent legal conclusions.

During downtime, store the packet as waiting for approved transport. Consumer email or a personal device is not an automatic fallback. When systems return, confirm that no one already transmitted the packet before initiating another send.

## Audit outbound custody

Review a sample from both the referral queue and the transport log. Confirm that each transmission links to authority evidence, a verified destination, an approved manifest, and the person who initiated it. Then look for approved packets with no transport result, failed sends without owners, repeated destination corrections, and acknowledgments that cannot be tied to a packet.

Counts can include packets stopped before send, destination mismatches, transport failures, and time from approval to first attempt. These indicators assess the handoff control. They do not show that the receiving organization provided services or that the referral was clinically appropriate.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a referral order automatically authorize record disclosure?

Not in every circumstance. The practice should have authorized personnel determine the applicable disclosure basis. Administrative staff should use the resulting documented instruction.

### Can consent be confirmed by a checkbox alone?

A checkbox is useful only when its meaning, source, subject, scope, and status are defined. Link it to the underlying evidence or approved system record rather than treating an unexplained flag as sufficient.

### What if the client changes the destination by phone?

Record the request exactly and send it through the practice's identity, consent, and destination-change process. Do not overwrite the prior destination before the change is authorized.

### Is a successful fax report the end of the referral?

It proves a transport result reported by the fax system. Whether that closes the handoff depends on the practice's requirement for acknowledgment or acceptance.

## Release only a reconciled packet

A reliable handoff ties a specific authority record to a verified recipient and an exact manifest, then preserves what happened in transport. HHS explains federal considerations in its [HIPAA disclosures for treatment guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/481/does-hipaa-permit-health-care-providers-to-share-information-for-treatment-purposes/index.html); practices should obtain appropriate guidance for their circumstances rather than asking coordinators to interpret it.
