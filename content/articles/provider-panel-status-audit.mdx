## Set the provider panel status scope

Audit Provider Panel Status Changes starts with a written boundary. Name the client, the source, the owner, and the evidence. A clear rule keeps routine coordination separate from clinical judgment.

The main risk is that a panel change can affect scheduling or billing without notice. Record the source and time before changing a status.

## Capture the evidence

A usable record includes provider, payer, panel, status, effective date, source, and owner. Keep the source beside the record so another reviewer can reconstruct what happened.

The credentialing lead confirms the decision or exception. The assistant can record changes, check sources, and route exceptions, but should not fill a gap with an assumption.

## Separate preparation from judgment

Decisions about credentialing and billing decisions remain with the authorized clinical or administrative owner. Use three labels: proceed, pause for missing evidence, and escalate.

## Run the review

Review stale status, missing notices, and rework. For example, a payer terminates a provider panel. Record what evidence was available and who owned the next step.

## Pilot one workflow

Start with one payer. Test the checklist on a small batch and review exceptions with the owner.
