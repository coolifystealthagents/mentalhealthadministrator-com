## How to approach: Track a no-show follow-up without blaming the client

A no-show follow-up should learn what happened and offer a next step, not assign blame. Start with the date, the appointment, and the contact attempt. A note that says client no-showed helps nobody. Write the deadline as a date and a local time.

Keep the follow-up short and respectful. The client may be embarrassed, overwhelmed, or unable to attend. The script should make it easy to return. If the source is unclear, route the question instead of guessing.

## Record the attempt and the barrier

Record the channel, the time, and the outcome of each attempt. If the client says transport was the problem, record the barrier in their words. Do not assume the reason. Test the rule on one real call before applying it to the queue.

Record the client's preference for the next contact. A client who asked for a text should not receive a call. The preference should be visible in the record. Write the deadline as a date and a local time.

## Offer the next step

Offer a realistic next step, such as a new appointment, a telehealth option, or a referral. Do not promise a slot the practice cannot staff. The client should know what to do next. Keep the evidence with the record rather than in a separate log.

Record the client's choice and the date. A follow-up that ends without a next step will produce another no-show. Keep the original note beside the corrected version. Ask whether a new reviewer could act on the record without a conversation.

## Protect privacy

Do not leave clinical detail in a voicemail or a text. Use a neutral message that asks the client to call the practice. The record should note the channel and the message. Update the template when the same question appears twice.

If the client cannot be reached, record the attempts and the next review. Do not keep calling without a plan. The practice should decide when to close the follow-up. Name the owner in the same line as the action.

## Review the no-shows monthly

Look for a pattern by day, time, clinician, or service. A repeated no-show at a certain time may point to a scheduling or access problem. Share the pattern with the practice manager. Review the first few notes together before the process expands.

Update the script or the reminder process. The goal is a client who can attend, not a longer list of missed appointments. Use the same format for every handover so the next reader can scan it. Name the owner in the same line as the action.
