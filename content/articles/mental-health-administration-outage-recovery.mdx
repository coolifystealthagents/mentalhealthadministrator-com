An outage creates two administrative problems: work must continue within approved limits, and temporary work must later be reconciled without loss or duplication. Recovery is not simply entering paper notes after the system returns. It is a controlled comparison between outage records, restored systems, external acknowledgments, and actions that may already have occurred.

## Quick overview:

- Declare the affected systems, start time, authorized continuity tools, and incident owner.
- Issue unique temporary identifiers and record every permitted action against them.
- Keep clinical or safety content on the practice's established escalation path.
- Restore by queue and risk of administrative harm, not by whichever stack is easiest.
- Close the incident only after discrepancies, duplicates, and unassigned work have owners.

## Start with an outage manifest

The manifest is the spine of recovery. Record the incident ID, discovery time, systems unavailable, functions affected, approved fallback locations, decision maker, staff notified, and time service is declared usable. A portal outage may affect incoming messages but not scheduling. An EHR outage may leave payer portals available while removing access to documentation. Describe these differences rather than using one blanket "system down" status.

Continuity records need a temporary ID, source channel, receipt time, minimum identity fields permitted by policy, request category, action taken, worker, next owner, and evidence location. Pre-numbered forms or a controlled electronic register make later completeness checks possible. Loose sticky notes do not.

HHS provides contingency planning material in its [HIPAA Security Rule guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/guidance/index.html). Practices should apply applicable requirements with qualified privacy, security, and legal guidance rather than treating a checklist as a certification.

## Control work during downtime

Not every normal task belongs in fallback operations. The incident plan should distinguish tasks that may continue, tasks that may be captured but not completed, and tasks that stop. A scheduler might record a callback request, while identity conflicts, disclosure decisions, coding changes, and clinical messages wait for or transfer to an authorized owner.

Maintain one action log. If a patient calls twice and an external partner also faxes, link the entries as possible duplicates without deleting either source. Record outbound attempts and their results. "Called" is incomplete if the procedure needs to distinguish connected, voicemail, failed number, or no answer.

Limit temporary records to information required for continuity. Use the practice-approved location and access list. Do not solve an outage by moving narratives to personal email, consumer messaging, or an uncontrolled spreadsheet.

## Restore in deliberate waves

First verify that the application is available and that interfaces, timestamps, document queues, and notifications are operating. A login screen alone does not prove data flow has recovered. Name who can authorize data entry to resume.

Then inventory the temporary IDs by workstream: appointments, messages, intake documents, referrals, authorizations, claims, and record requests. For each item:

1. locate or create the permanent record using approved identity controls;
2. link the outage ID to the permanent identifier;
3. compare source timestamps and current status;
4. enter only information supported by the outage record;
5. identify actions already completed elsewhere;
6. route conflicts to the correct owner; and
7. mark reconciliation with worker and time.

Use a second-person check for duplicates, unmatched people, sensitive disclosures, and any item whose chronology affects the next action. The checker confirms reconciliation evidence, not clinical correctness.

## Resolve collisions instead of overwriting them

Imagine that a person requested cancellation during the outage, while an automated reminder service later recorded confirmation. Preserve both events and their times. Do not choose the newest value automatically, because delayed interfaces can distort sequence. Assign the scheduling conflict for review and retain the resulting decision source.

Other common collisions include two temporary IDs for one request, a paper intake packet also uploaded through the restored portal, and payer status received before the internal claim record is accessible. Link first, compare next, then let the authorized owner determine the supported state.

## Decide when recovery is complete

An incident can leave routine operations while a residual exception list remains open. That list needs item ID, discrepancy, impact, owner, next review date, and closure evidence. Do not declare all work recovered while unmatched temporary records sit in a drawer.

Useful counts include temporary IDs issued, IDs reconciled, duplicates linked, unmatched identities, failed interfaces, and open exceptions by age. Publish the extraction time and exclusions. These are recovery-control indicators, not claims about patient outcomes.

A brief after-action review should ask which fallback form fields were missing, whether staff knew where to store records, what notification failed, and which queues were hardest to reconstruct. Convert each finding into an owner and test date.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should staff back-enter every temporary note?

No. Enter what the approved permanent record requires, preserve the source reference, and avoid duplicating unsupported or unnecessary narrative. Follow the practice's retention and correction rules.

### What if the outage start time is uncertain?

Record the earliest confirmed failure, the last known successful event, and the uncertainty. Search affected interfaces and queues across that interval rather than inventing a precise start time.

### Can the outage close with exceptions still open?

The technology incident may close while a controlled residual queue remains. Every residual item should have an accountable owner, next action, and review date.

## Recover evidence, not just access

A resilient practice can account for what arrived, what happened, and what still needs attention across the outage boundary. [Provider calendar governance](/blog/provider-calendar-template-governance) supports restoration of scheduling rules, while [EHR access offboarding](/blog/mental-health-ehr-access-offboarding) helps keep fallback and restored access limited to current workers. Recovery ends with reconciled custody, not merely a green system status.
