An intake exception queue should identify a specific documentary problem, not label a person "incomplete." The queue exists to make the missing or unusable administrative item visible, send an approved request when appropriate, and route questions that require legal, clinical, financial, or accommodation judgment.

## Quick overview:

- Define required items by service and current form version.
- Use distinct reasons for missing, unreadable, unsigned, expired, mismatched, and unreviewed material.
- Store the document once and place a reference in the queue.
- Give every exception a next action, owner, and review date.
- Never infer a response or complete a person's attestation.

## Build the requirement matrix first

A queue cannot be reliable if staff do not know what constitutes an expected item. Maintain a versioned matrix by intake pathway, service, location, age or authority category when applicable, and payer workflow. Name the owner who approves each requirement. Administrative staff apply the matrix; they do not decide which consent, disclosure authority, or clinical questionnaire is legally or clinically sufficient.

When requirements change, record the effective date and how in-progress intakes are treated. Otherwise, a new form can make every older packet appear defective even when it followed the prior instruction.

The HHS [minimum necessary guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/minimum-necessary-requirement/index.html) provides context for limiting uses, disclosures, and requests for protected health information. Practice owners should define intake access and routing rules, while intake teams route privacy or disclosure questions rather than improvise legal answers.

## Use reason codes that reveal the repair

"Missing document" is too broad. Better exception types include:

| Reason | Evidence | Routine action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| not received | expected-item check and source search | approved reminder |
| unreadable or corrupt | failed rendering or illegible page | request replacement |
| signature absent | required signature field blank | return current form |
| identity mismatch | conflicting identifiers | pause and route review |
| wrong form version | received and required versions | apply transition rule |
| attachment misfiled | source exists under wrong record | restricted correction process |
| protected review pending | document received, decision outstanding | assign authorized reviewer |

Do not use "not received" until approved source locations have been checked. A portal upload, fax, scanned batch, referral attachment, and secure email may represent the same file. Link duplicates rather than retaining multiple uncontrolled copies.

## Design the queue row for action

Include intake ID, person record status, service pathway, expected item code, exception reason, date detected, source locations checked, current form version, outreach permission and channel, last attempt result, next action date, owner, escalation reason, and closure evidence. The queue should reference the approved document repository instead of embedding sensitive forms in comments.

Separate "unknown" from "not applicable." Only an authorized rule or reviewer should mark a requirement not applicable. If staff cannot verify identity or authority, the row remains unresolved and moves to the designated owner.

## Work by dependency, not just age

A useful view groups items according to what can happen next. Some missing demographic fields can be requested with a standard message. A garbled insurance image belongs to a replacement request. A response containing clinical details should stop the clerical script and follow the practice's message process. A custody or representative question requires authorized review before outreach or disclosure.

Consider a packet with a signed demographic form, an unreadable insurance image, and a questionnaire that includes a safety statement. Create the insurance replacement task, preserve the questionnaire in the approved record, and route its content under the safety procedure. Do not hold the safety handoff until the image is replaced, and do not treat the entire packet as one generic exception.

## Prevent reminder loops

Set an outreach schedule approved by the practice, with attempt result values such as delivered, failed, returned, no response, and contact restricted. A sent message is not proof the person received it. Respect current contact preferences and stop rules. Repeated reminders generated from duplicate rows create confusion and can expose unnecessary information.

Review old items for the reason they remain open. The answer may be no assigned owner, a failed upload link, a stale form URL, or an unresolved policy question. Fixing the source problem is more useful than asking staff to clear more rows.

Quality checks should compare selected rows to the requirement matrix and original source. Count exceptions by reason, pathway, form version, and source channel. Also count rows closed as duplicates or not applicable. These measures describe document administration, not readiness for care or clinical eligibility.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can staff fill a blank field from another document?

Only if an approved procedure identifies that source and permits the clerical transfer. Staff should not infer, reinterpret, or complete an attestation on someone's behalf.

### When is an exception closed?

Closure requires the corrected item, an authorized not-applicable decision, a linked duplicate, or another defined disposition. Sending a reminder is an action, not closure.

### What if a form contains unexpected clinical information?

Preserve the original in the approved system and follow the practice's clinical or safety routing procedure. Do not summarize or evaluate it in the exception queue.

## Make the defect specific

The strongest queue tells staff exactly which item failed which check and who can resolve it. Connect upstream arrivals through [referral source reconciliation](/blog/mental-health-referral-source-reconciliation), then coordinate permitted reminders with [waitlist contact preferences](/blog/behavioral-health-waitlist-contact-preferences). A specific exception supports correction; a generic incomplete label merely moves uncertainty around.
