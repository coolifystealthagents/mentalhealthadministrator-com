## Set the waitlist contact scope

Record Client Waitlist Contact Preferences starts with a written boundary. Name the client, the source, the owner, and the evidence. A clear rule keeps routine coordination separate from clinical judgment.

The main risk is that a client can be contacted by the wrong channel or after opting out. Record the source and time before changing a status.

## Capture the evidence

A usable record includes client, waitlist, channel, consent, attempts, owner, and status. Keep the source beside the record so another reviewer can reconstruct what happened.

The scheduling lead confirms the decision or exception. The assistant can record preferences, send approved outreach, and route clinical questions, but should not fill a gap with an assumption.

## Separate preparation from judgment

Decisions about clinical triage and scheduling decisions remain with the authorized clinical or administrative owner. Use three labels: proceed, pause for missing evidence, and escalate.

## Run the review

Review contact completion, opt-outs, and open cases. For example, a client asks for text only. Record what evidence was available and who owned the next step.

## Pilot one workflow

Start with one waitlist. Test the checklist on a small batch and review exceptions with the owner.
