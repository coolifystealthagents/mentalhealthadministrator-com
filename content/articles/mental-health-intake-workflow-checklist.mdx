A mental health intake workflow should show how an inquiry becomes an organized record for authorized practice review. It should identify every entry channel, the minimum administrative data to collect, the materials to send, the evidence required at each handoff, and the conditions that close or continue the record. It must also keep a firm boundary between checking whether a field is present and interpreting what the person wrote.

A nonclinical remote assistant can log an inquiry, send approved forms, verify designated administrative fields, request a named missing item, update workflow status, and route a packet. The assistant cannot assess symptoms, assign urgency, determine diagnosis, promise acceptance, or choose a treatment path. Those questions belong to qualified and authorized practice personnel.

## Quick overview:

- Consolidate every inquiry channel into one owned administrative record.
- Separate receipt and field completeness from clinical review and acceptance decisions.
- Preserve evidence at each handoff until scheduling, transfer, or supported closure.

## Map every way an inquiry enters the practice

Start with the actual channels in use: telephone, voicemail, web form, portal, referral feed, approved email account, or in person request. Each channel needs a collection owner and a rule for moving the inquiry into one source of truth. Personal notes and private spreadsheets create blind spots because another team member cannot reliably see custody or continue the work.

### Decide what the first record must contain

The opening record should be limited to fields the practice has approved. Common administrative fields may include a unique inquiry identifier, received date, contact details, contact permission, requested service in the person's own terms, referral source, form delivery channel, current owner, and next review time. Staff should not probe for clinical narrative simply to make the tracker feel complete.

If an entry channel collects information beyond what administrative staff need, configure access where possible so the designated reviewer receives that material directly. Minimum access should reflect the assigned task, not the total amount of information available in the system.

The [Office of the National Coordinator for Health Information Technology](https://www.healthit.gov/topic/privacy-security-and-hipaa/security-risk-assessment-tool) provides security risk assessment resources that can inform a practice review. The resource does not certify a particular workflow. Legal, contractual, security, and operational decisions remain with the practice and its advisers.

## Give intake statuses observable meanings

A status is useful only when two staff members would apply it to the same facts. “In progress” is too broad. A more informative sequence might distinguish new inquiry, packet sent, administrative items missing, packet received, ready for authorized review, decision recorded, communication pending, scheduled, and closed.

### Forms received does not mean accepted

Receipt indicates only that the designated materials arrived. Administrative completeness indicates that specified fields, signatures, and documents are present. Ready for review means the packet has entered the authorized review queue. None of those states says that the person is appropriate for a service or that the practice has agreed to provide care.

Each state should have entry criteria, an owner, expected next action, and exit criteria. If a packet cannot advance, the status should reveal why without disclosing unnecessary sensitive detail. For example, “administrative item missing” can link to a controlled field naming the requested item.

## Run the intake sequence with controlled handoffs

The exact sequence will vary, but a practice can test its design against the following flow:

1. Capture the inquiry in the approved register and assign an owner.
2. Check recorded contact permission before sending material.
3. Send the current packet through the permitted channel and record its version.
4. Match returned materials to the correct inquiry.
5. Check only the administrative elements listed in the procedure.
6. Move discrepancies or sensitive exceptions to the designated queue.
7. Route a complete packet to the authorized practice reviewer.
8. Communicate only the next step that the authorized decision permits.
9. Schedule, continue follow-up, or close with a supported reason.

This is a custody sequence, not a clinical screening tool. If a questionnaire answer appears concerning or ambiguous, an assistant should follow the practice escalation route rather than classify it or ask diagnostic follow-up questions.

### Evidence fields that support a reliable handoff

| Field | Evidence captured |
| --- | --- |
| Inquiry ID | Stable reference across channels and documents |
| Received timestamp | When the practice obtained the inquiry |
| Entry source | Which approved channel created the record |
| Contact preference source | Where permission and channel instructions came from |
| Packet version and sent time | Which materials were issued and when |
| Items returned | Administrative inventory without interpretation |
| Exception category | The reason ordinary processing paused |
| Current custodian | Person or queue responsible now |
| Authorized decision source | Who recorded the permitted next step |
| Next action date | When the open record must be reviewed again |
| Closure reason | Documented endpoint for completed intake work |

Evidence should remain in approved systems. A handoff note can point to the controlled document rather than reproduce a sensitive response in a separate message.

## Build an exception path for incomplete and conflicting material

Exceptions deserve a visible queue rather than informal problem solving. Typical administrative exceptions include an unmatched form, duplicate inquiry, missing signature, unreadable attachment, inconsistent contact detail, failed delivery, or a request that exceeds the assistant's authority. The related [mental health intake document exception queue](/blog/mental-health-intake-document-exception-queue) article covers this custody problem in more detail.

### Example: a form is complete except for a clinical answer

If the practice procedure says a clinical section must be reviewed by a designated professional, the assistant should not ask the person to rewrite the response or decide that it is adequate. Record that the packet was routed for authorized review and identify the queue. The administrative check can still report whether the form and signature are present without evaluating the answer.

### Example: two inquiries appear to concern the same person

Do not merge records based on name alone. Hold outreach that could create duplicate messages, record the matching facts allowed by the procedure, and send the possible duplicate to the authorized resolver. Preserve source history. Once identity and record handling are confirmed, document which record remains active and what happened to the duplicate under practice policy.

## Specify when an intake record may close

Closure conditions prevent old inquiries from disappearing without explanation. An intake item may close after scheduling is confirmed, an authorized practice decision is communicated, the person withdraws through an approved channel, the permitted contact sequence is completed without response, a duplicate is reconciled, or custody transfers to another documented process.

A closure entry should include the reason code, evidence source, timestamp, person applying the status, and any retention or follow-up instruction. If clinical, privacy, legal, or safety content has been routed, the intake task should not be marked complete until the receiving owner accepts custody or records the applicable resolution. Closing a row is not a substitute for completing a handoff.

## Evaluate the process with administrative questions

Supervisors can examine whether every open item has an owner, packets use current versions, missing item requests match the approved list, documents are attached to the correct inquiry, and review queues acknowledge receipt. Aging reports can reveal records with no next action. Exception categories can reveal a broken web form or confusing instruction.

These observations describe workflow health, not clinical effectiveness. The assistant should not be asked to score the quality of responses, evaluate need, or infer why a person stopped replying. Quality review should focus on authorized actions, accurate evidence, timely custody, and adherence to boundaries.

## Intake launch and maintenance checklist

* Inventory inquiry channels and eliminate hidden collection points.
* Choose the source of truth and assign collection coverage.
* Define status entry and exit criteria in plain language.
* Approve forms, message templates, versions, and channel rules.
* Identify which fields are administrative and which require authorized review.
* Create routes for duplicate, conflicting, sensitive, and urgent content.
* Test handoffs during absence and after normal business hours.
* Document attempt limits, closure reasons, and custody acceptance.
* Sample records after changing forms, services, systems, or staffing.

Where an inquiry starts as an outside referral, connect the same identity and custody rules to the [referral tracking workflow](/blog/referral-tracking-for-mental-health-practices). Avoid recreating the whole intake packet in a second tracker.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a virtual assistant perform mental health intake?

A nonclinical assistant may support the administrative intake sequence, including form delivery, designated completeness checks, data entry, and routing. Clinical assessment, urgency, diagnosis, treatment fit, and care decisions stay with qualified practice personnel.

### Which intake statuses should a practice use?

Use only the states needed to make real handoffs visible. Each status should state what fact is established, who owns the item, and what moves it forward. The practice should adapt the vocabulary to its tools and services.

### Should administrative staff read every intake answer?

Access should match the task. If an answer is not needed for the assigned administrative check, the workflow should route it to the appropriate reviewer without asking the assistant to interpret it.

### How should an unresolved inquiry be closed?

Apply the practice approved attempt sequence and intervals, document each result, and use a specific closure reason. Do not use a generic closed label while an exception or accepted handoff remains outstanding.

## Make the first handoff understandable

A dependable intake process gives the next person enough evidence to act without asking administrative staff to make clinical judgments. Trace several recent inquiries from arrival to endpoint, note where ownership became unclear, and rewrite those transitions. That exercise creates a practical foundation for any remote intake support role.
