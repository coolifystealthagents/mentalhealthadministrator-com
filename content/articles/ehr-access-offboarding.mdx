## Set the EHR offboarding scope

Complete EHR Access Offboarding starts with a written boundary. Name the client, the source, the owner, and the evidence. A clear rule keeps routine coordination separate from clinical judgment.

The main risk is that a departing user can retain access to clinical records. Record the source and time before changing a status.

## Capture the evidence

A usable record includes user, role, last day, access, records, owner, and confirmation. Keep the source beside the record so another reviewer can reconstruct what happened.

The access lead confirms the decision or exception. The assistant can track removal, confirm completion, and route exceptions, but should not fill a gap with an assumption.

## Separate preparation from judgment

Decisions about access and clinical decisions remain with the authorized clinical or administrative owner. Use three labels: proceed, pause for missing evidence, and escalate.

## Run the review

Review active accounts, missing confirmation, and incomplete transfer. For example, a clinician leaves the practice. Record what evidence was available and who owned the next step.

## Pilot one workflow

Start with one role. Test the checklist on a small batch and review exceptions with the owner.
