Portal proxy access lets one person use their own account to interact with another person's record under an approved relationship and scope. It should never be implemented by sharing the client's password or by informally changing the email address on the client's account. The administrative workflow must connect authority evidence, identity checks, system configuration, and later revocation.

## Quick overview:

- Keep the subject, proxy, authority basis, scope, and effective period distinct.
- Verify identities through the practice's approved process before sending an invitation.
- Configure the narrowest portal role that matches the approved instruction.
- Test what the proxy can actually see and do, including messaging and billing features.
- Revoke or reassess access when authority, age, relationship, or system status changes.

## Define the access object before creating it

A proxy record should include the client's record ID, proxy's separate identity ID, relationship as documented, authority evidence reference, approving role, permitted portal role, restricted functions, effective date, end or review date, invitation destination, and status history.

Do not place the proxy's email into the client's login profile merely to make enrollment work. Each person needs an attributable account. Shared credentials defeat reliable access logs and make selective revocation difficult.

The [HHS guidance on personal representatives](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/personal-representatives/index.html) describes circumstances in which a person may act on an individual's behalf. Applying authority to a specific portal and record can involve legal, privacy, clinical, and technical questions, so those decisions belong to the practice's designated reviewers.

## Separate evidence collection from authority decisions

Administrative staff may provide the current request form, collect required fields, verify receipt, compare identifiers under a script, and route the packet. They should not interpret legal documents, determine whether a person is a personal representative, resolve a contested relationship, or choose which sensitive information should be visible.

Use reason-coded stops:

- subject identity does not match the record;
- proxy identity cannot be verified;
- email or phone belongs to another account;
- authority document is missing, expired, altered, or unclear;
- multiple people claim conflicting authority;
- requested scope is broader than available roles;
- the record has a restriction requiring special review;
- the request includes a safety or clinical concern.

The exception task should carry the request ID and evidence references, not full document copies in an unrestricted queue.

## Map approved scope to real portal capabilities

Portal labels such as `full`, `limited`, or `caregiver` can hide many permissions. Maintain a capability matrix by software version:

| Capability | Questions for the owner |
| --- | --- |
| view documents | which document classes and dates appear? |
| messaging | can the proxy initiate, receive, or impersonate? |
| scheduling | can the proxy book, cancel, or see reasons? |
| billing | can the proxy view balances or statements? |
| forms | whose signature and identity are recorded? |
| downloads | what can be exported, and is proxy action logged? |

If the approved scope cannot be represented, stop and return the mismatch to the access owner. Do not grant a broader role with a note asking the proxy not to use some features. A note is not a technical restriction.

Recheck the matrix after portal upgrades. A release may expose a newly added document type or enable messaging by default. Product testing should use approved test identities, not a real client record.

## Issue an invitation without weakening identity controls

Before sending, confirm the proxy contact destination from the approved request and check for duplicate or conflicting portal accounts. Record the invitation ID, sender, time, role, expiration, and delivery result. An invitation marked sent is not activated access.

Activation should occur through the portal's identity process. Staff should not accept passwords, answer challenge questions for the proxy, or bypass controls because enrollment is difficult. Route accessibility or technical barriers to the designated support process.

After activation, verify the actual assigned role and subject relationship in the administration console. When the system offers a proxy-facing confirmation, ensure it identifies whose record is accessible and avoids unnecessary clinical detail.

## Manage lifecycle events explicitly

Proxy access is not a set-and-forget field. Define review triggers that match the practice's environment, such as:

- the documented authority reaches an end date;
- the client reaches an age or status that requires reassessment;
- the client or authorized owner requests revocation;
- the proxy's identity or contact information changes;
- the relationship is disputed;
- the portal changes roles or capabilities;
- the client record is merged or corrected;
- suspicious activity is reported.

A revocation task should identify every relevant portal relationship, pending invitation, connected application, and open proxy-generated message. Record the disable time and result. Deciding how to handle an active clinical message is for the appropriate owner, not the access administrator.

Do not silently reactivate a prior proxy after an account migration. Reconcile authority and role under the current procedure.

## Detect and correct boundary failures

A common failure occurs when an invitation goes to an address already associated with another person. Another is a correct role applied to the wrong client record. Freeze the relationship, preserve access logs and transaction IDs, and notify the named privacy and system owners under the incident procedure.

Do not delete audit events or edit demographics to conceal the error. Separate correction from any determination about notification, disclosure, or harm. Those determinations need authorized review.

For routine quality checks, sample newly activated and revoked relationships. Compare the authority reference, approved scope, configured role, invitation destination, activation identity, and current status. Also inspect dormant invitations and relationships overdue for review.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a family member use the client's username and password?

No. A proxy should use a separate attributable account and the portal's approved proxy mechanism.

### Does collecting a form authorize staff to approve the proxy?

Not unless the worker is explicitly assigned that authority. Collection and completeness review can be separated from the authority decision.

### What if the portal's limited role is still too broad?

Do not configure it as a workaround. Document the capability gap and ask the access owner to select another approved method.

### Should access disappear immediately after revocation?

The practice should define its expected timing and escalation path. Record when the request was received, when technical access ended, and any systems that require separate action.

## Make access technically match the approved relationship

The strongest proxy record links a verified person, reviewed authority, explicit scope, exact system role, and lifecycle history. It allows support staff to administer access without interpreting authority or disclosure rules.
