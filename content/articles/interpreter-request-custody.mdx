## Set the interpreter requests scope

Track Interpreter Requests starts with a written boundary. Name the client, the source, the owner, and the evidence. A clear rule keeps routine coordination separate from clinical judgment.

The main risk is that an interpreter can be booked for the wrong time or modality. Record the source and time before changing a status.

## Capture the evidence

A usable record includes client, language, modality, appointment, vendor, confirmation, and owner. Keep the source beside the record so another reviewer can reconstruct what happened.

The scheduling lead confirms the decision or exception. The assistant can book the approved service, confirm, and route changes, but should not fill a gap with an assumption.

## Separate preparation from judgment

Decisions about clinical and language access decisions remain with the authorized clinical or administrative owner. Use three labels: proceed, pause for missing evidence, and escalate.

## Run the review

Review missing interpreters, wrong modality, and late changes. For example, a session needs a Spanish interpreter. Record what evidence was available and who owned the next step.

## Pilot one workflow

Start with one appointment type. Test the checklist on a small batch and review exceptions with the owner.
