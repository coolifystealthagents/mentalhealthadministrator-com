Prior authorization administration depends on current payer instructions, an approved source for every submitted field, and a tracker that distinguishes a request from a determination. A checklist can organize this work, but it cannot decide what clinical information is appropriate, create a rationale, interpret medical necessity, or predict whether a payer will approve a service.

A nonclinical remote assistant may verify identifiers, obtain current forms from an authorized source, assemble materials supplied or approved by practice personnel, record submission confirmation, monitor payer stated status, and route notices. Diagnoses, treatment information, clinical answers, appeal choices, and responses requiring professional judgment remain with authorized practice staff.

## Quick overview:

- Verify the current payer requirement and submission channel for the specific request.
- Keep clinical answers and rationale with the authorized practice professional who supplied them.
- Track packet contents, submission proof, payer notices, deadlines, and final custody separately.

## Open the request with a verified administrative context

Before gathering documents, identify the request precisely. The tracker should connect the correct person, payer, plan, proposed service, rendering details where applicable, and relevant dates. Use practice designated sources rather than assumptions based on a previous request. Similar plan names or an old card image may not establish the current process.

### Keep eligibility and authorization distinct

An eligibility response, benefit description, authorization requirement, authorization determination, and payment decision are separate records. Do not let a tracker label blur them together. If the team records an eligibility response, preserve its source and date without treating it as a guarantee. The [behavioral health eligibility response provenance](/blog/behavioral-health-eligibility-response-provenance) article explores that evidence chain.

The [Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services prior authorization overview](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/burden-reduction/interoperability/prior-authorization) describes federal initiatives and resources. It does not provide a universal procedure for every payer, plan, service, or jurisdiction. Teams should consult the sources and guidance that govern the specific request.

## Check the current requirement instead of copying the last packet

Payer instructions can vary. Record where the requirement was checked, the date and time, the applicable service or request type, the forms identified, the accepted submission channel, and any reference supplied by the payer. An old checklist can help staff remember questions, but it should not be treated as current authority.

### Divide the form by authority

Mark fields as verified administrative entry, clinical input required, practice approval required, or payer generated. Administrative staff may enter identifiers from approved records. A licensed or otherwise authorized practice person should supply diagnoses, clinical narratives, treatment information, and other content that requires professional judgment. The assistant should not adapt language from a past request to fill a gap.

If a portal forces completion of an unclear clinical question, pause the submission and route a screenshot or field reference through the approved channel. Technical ability to type into a field is not permission to answer it.

## Assemble a packet with a submission manifest

A packet manifest is the record of exactly what was sent. It can list the form version, document names, document dates, approving source, page count where useful, submission channel, and submission timestamp. Review file legibility and matching before release. Do not add extra records on the theory that more information will improve the outcome.

### Evidence fields for the authorization tracker

| Field | Required evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Internal request ID | Stable link to practice records |
| Payer and plan | Source used to identify the applicable process |
| Service and date context | Administrative description approved by the practice |
| Requirement source | Portal page, payer instruction, or authorized contact |
| Requirement checked at | Timestamp for the current verification |
| Clinical content owner | Authorized person supplying or approving clinical fields |
| Packet manifest | Forms and attachments actually transmitted |
| Submission proof | Confirmation, reference number, fax result, or portal receipt |
| Payer stated status | Exact administrative state reported by the source |
| Requested follow-up | Additional item or action reported by the payer |
| Next review date | When the item returns to the queue |
| Determination custody | Person responsible for interpreting and acting on the notice |

The tracker should point to protected source material rather than duplicate clinical content. Status notes should quote or accurately record the payer's administrative wording without translating it into a promise.

## Track the request without forecasting the result

After submission, assign an owner and a review date. Each status check should record the channel, timestamp, reference, payer stated state, and any named missing item. “Pending” should not become “likely approved,” and verbal information should not replace a formal notice when the practice process requires one.

### Example: the payer asks for additional clinical information

The assistant records the request as stated, preserves the notice or call reference, and assigns it to the clinical content owner. The assistant does not choose records, draft a justification, or explain why the service is necessary. After authorized personnel supply the response, the assistant may transmit it if that action is within the approved role and the manifest is updated.

### Example: a portal shows conflicting statuses

If one screen says received and another says incomplete, capture the observable conflict and contact the authorized payer channel according to procedure. Keep the request in an exception state with a next review time. Do not select the more favorable status or tell the client that approval exists.

## Route determinations and deadline events to their owners

A determination notice should move promptly to the person authorized to interpret it and choose any next step. Administrative staff can preserve the receipt time, notice date, reference, and stated deadline. They should not recommend an appeal, explain clinical implications, or conduct peer review.

The related guide to [track payer peer review deadlines without clinical participation](/blog/track-payer-peer-review-deadlines-without-clinical-participation) separates calendar and custody work from professional participation. Expiration dates also require their own controls and a separately owned downstream queue.

## Define closure by outcome and custody

An authorization task may close when a formal determination has been received and accepted by the responsible practice owner, when the practice withdraws the request through an authorized decision, when the payer confirms no request is required and that evidence is stored, or when the work transfers to a separately owned appeal or expiration process.

Each closure entry should retain the reason, supporting source, timestamp, final owner, and any linked next process. A denial is not merely a generic closed item if a deadline remains under review. A submission is not complete if confirmation is missing or a portal error leaves transmission uncertain. Closure means the administrative chain is accounted for, not that the payer produced a favorable result.

## Audit the workflow without making clinical judgments

Reviewers can compare the requirement source with the packet manifest, confirm that clinical fields have an authorized source, verify submission evidence, and identify requests with expired next action dates. They can also look for stale copied forms, duplicate submissions, missing determinations, or notices without accepted custody.

Counts of pending requests, exception age, or missing confirmation show the condition of administrative work. They do not establish whether care is appropriate, whether authorization should be granted, or whether payment will occur. Present operational reporting with those limits intact.

## Prior authorization operating checklist

* Verify person, payer, plan, service, and relevant dates.
* Check the current requirement through a designated source.
* Record source location and verification timestamp.
* Assign every clinical field to authorized practice personnel.
* Build and approve the exact packet manifest.
* Preserve transmission confirmation and payer references.
* Give every pending item an owner and next review date.
* Route contradictory information into an exception state.
* Deliver determinations and deadline notices to named owners.
* Close only after outcome evidence and custody are documented.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a remote assistant submit a prior authorization request?

A practice may assign controlled clerical submission when payer rules, contracts, access, supervision, and practice policy permit it. Clinical content must come from appropriately authorized practice personnel.

### What should never be copied from an older request automatically?

Do not reuse diagnoses, treatment details, clinical narratives, signatures, attestations, payer requirements, or dates without a current authorized source. A prior packet may not describe the current request.

### Does an authorization mean the claim will be paid?

No guarantee should be given. Eligibility, benefits, authorization, claim processing, and payment involve distinct rules and decisions. Administrative staff should report the exact documented state.

### When can the authorization tracker mark an item complete?

Completion requires a defined endpoint, supporting evidence, and accepted custody for any remaining action. Submission alone usually moves the request into tracking rather than closing it.

## Make every request reconstructable

The most useful authorization record lets a reviewer see which requirement governed, who supplied clinical content, what crossed the payer channel, and who received the response. Build that trace before increasing request volume. A bounded remote role can then support clerical coordination without taking over decisions that belong to the practice or payer.
