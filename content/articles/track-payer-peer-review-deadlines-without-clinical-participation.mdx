A payer peer review request combines two different jobs. One is administrative: capture the request, calculate or transcribe the operational deadline under approved rules, arrange contact, and preserve outcomes. The other is professional: discuss clinical information and medical necessity. A well-designed tracker supports the first job without pulling administrative staff into the second.

## Quick overview:

Open a deadline record from the payer's notice or documented call. Preserve the wording, time zone, contact route, case reference, named clinician owner, and any available scheduling windows. Escalate conflicting or unclear dates instead of choosing one. Administrative staff can coordinate availability and document attempts, but the clinician controls clinical preparation, discussion, and conclusions.

## Capture the request as received

The intake record should include payer name, case or authorization reference, received timestamp, source channel, exact deadline wording, stated time zone, payer contact instructions, callback requirements, request type as labeled by the payer, source artifact, assigned clinician, operational coordinator, and current state.

Keep "payer-stated deadline" separate from "practice action target." An internal target may be earlier to allow scheduling, but it must not replace the payer value. If a fax says "within two business days" and the portal displays a calendar date, retain both. Record any approved conversion rule and the person or role that resolved ambiguity.

## Run two linked lanes

The **coordination lane** covers receipt, assignment, clinician availability, payer contact attempts, conference confirmation, reminder delivery, and evidence of completion or cancellation. The **clinical lane** belongs to the qualified clinician and includes record review, clinical statements, medical-necessity discussion, and care decisions.

Link the lanes by a case identifier, not by copying clinical preparation into the administrative tracker. The coordinator may report that required materials are with the clinician if the approved system supports that state. The coordinator should not summarize those materials for the payer or answer substantive questions while arranging the call.

## Make the clock explicit

Deadline handling should answer: When did the clock start? Which time zone applies? Are business days defined? Does the payer specify a call-by time or merely a request window? What happens around holidays? Who can decide when language is unclear?

Do not rely on a calendar reminder alone. The queue should surface unassigned requests, deadlines with no time zone, clinician acknowledgment pending, payer callback pending, and deadlines approaching under the practice's approved thresholds. A backup coordinator may maintain the queue, but only an appropriately authorized clinician should replace the assigned clinical participant.

A deadline correction must preserve the earlier value and source. If a payer representative supplies a new date, record the representative or call reference, time, and exact statement. Never move the date merely to remove an overdue flag.

## Coordinate without joining the clinical exchange

Administrative staff can offer availability from an approved calendar, provide the practice's contact details, confirm the payer's number, arrange a conference, and log connection attempts. At the start of a call, they may perform the minimum introduction or transfer allowed by procedure, then leave the clinical exchange if that is the approved setup.

They should not present a clinical rationale, relay diagnoses beyond an authorized administrative identifier, answer questions about symptoms or treatment, coach the clinician's argument, or document clinical conclusions in a general operations board. If the payer begins asking substantive questions before transfer, state the role boundary and connect the authorized clinician.

## Use evidence-rich statuses

Suggested states include request captured, source conflict, clinician assignment pending, clinician acknowledged, payer scheduling pending, conference booked, connection attempted, completed awaiting payer outcome, payer outcome received, deadline missed under review, and closed.

Each status requires evidence. "Conference booked" should point to date, time, time zone, participants, and confirmation source. "Connection attempted" should record the number or channel, time, reported result, and next action. "Completed" can be based on clinician confirmation or another approved source, but it should not reveal the clinical discussion in the coordination queue.

## Prepare for difficult scenarios

**Verbal window conflicts with portal:** Keep both deadlines and route the discrepancy immediately. Until resolved, use the practice's conservative escalation procedure without representing it as payer policy.

**Clinician unavailable:** Notify the named backup process. The coordinator does not select a substitute based solely on an open calendar because credentials, involvement, or payer requirements may matter.

**Payer does not answer:** Record each approved attempt and contact route. Follow the payer-specific or practice escalation procedure. An attempted call is not a completed review.

**Request arrives after its stated cutoff:** Preserve receipt evidence and route it for authorized payer follow-up. Do not alter timestamps or tell the client what the payer will decide.

**Clinical content lands in the tracker:** Move or restrict it according to practice procedure while preserving appropriate audit history. Review why the form invited unnecessary narrative.

## Review missed and near-missed records

Audit from the source notice through final state. Check intake timing, deadline transcription, time-zone treatment, clinician acknowledgment, attempts, conference evidence, payer outcome routing, and corrections. Examine near misses even if the call occurred, since they often reveal single-person dependencies or notification failures.

Operational counts may include requests with incomplete deadline data, assignment lag, confirmed conferences, payer no-answer events, source conflicts, and missed stated deadlines. Publish definitions and exclusions. Do not present completed calls as overturned decisions, payment success, or improved care.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a coordinator remain on the peer review call?

Only if the practice has explicitly defined an authorized administrative purpose and appropriate boundaries. The coordinator should not participate in clinical discussion or create an unnecessary copy of it.

### What if the payer asks the coordinator a clinical question?

Decline to answer and transfer or route the question to the assigned qualified clinician. Document the administrative handoff, not a guessed response.

### Which deadline wins when sources disagree?

Administrative staff should not decide. Preserve each source and timestamp, flag the conflict, and obtain a documented determination from the designated owner.

### Does a completed call prove authorization?

No. It proves only that the tracked call occurred under the chosen evidence rule. Record a later payer determination as a separate event and avoid promising coverage or payment.

## Protect the boundary while protecting the date

The strongest process gives coordinators enough detail to manage the clock and no invitation to practice clinically. CMS publishes background on [health plan prior authorization processes](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/burden-reduction/interoperability), while payer instructions and the practice's approved procedures govern each tracked case.
