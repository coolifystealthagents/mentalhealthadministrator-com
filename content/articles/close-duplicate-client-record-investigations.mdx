Two records with the same name are not necessarily duplicates, and two records with different names may belong to the same person. A safe investigation contains the suspected pair, compares approved identity evidence, maps dependent transactions, and reserves merge or unmerge authority for designated record owners. The goal is not simply fewer records. It is one defensible disposition with no lost provenance.

## Quick overview:

- Open a case for the suspected record pair or set before changing either record.
- Prevent new cross-posting while ordinary care and administration continue under local instructions.
- Compare approved identifiers and document conflicts without guessing.
- Inventory appointments, documents, messages, balances, portal access, and outside interfaces before a merge.
- Require authorized review, validation, and a correction plan that reflects the system's documented reversibility limits.

## Contain the suspected pair

Assign an investigation ID and link the candidate record identifiers. Apply the approved warning or work-queue status so staff know a review is active. A warning should not expose unnecessary details or prevent permitted work. It should tell the next worker which record owner to contact and which actions are paused.

Containment usually means no casual demographic consolidation, no copying documents between charts, no portal invitation based on the unverified record, and no deletion. If an appointment or communication must proceed, staff need a documented path for choosing a temporary operational record without deciding the final identity question.

Duplicate detection scores are leads, not conclusions. A name, birth month, postal code, or phone number may be shared or stale. Record the trigger, algorithm or report version when applicable, detection time, and person who opened the case.

## Compare evidence field by field

Use an approved identity comparison worksheet. Keep the actual values in the restricted record system and show only match states in broader workflow views.

| Element | Comparison state |
| --- | --- |
| legal or recorded name | exact, variant, conflict, unavailable |
| date of birth | match, conflict, partial, unavailable |
| contact details | match, prior value, shared, conflict |
| internal identifiers | distinct, reused, migration artifact |
| payer identifiers | match, conflict, plan changed |
| portal identity | linked, separate, unknown |
| document signatures | review required, not compared |

No single weak identifier should force a merge. Also distinguish a demographic correction from identity resolution. A typo in one record may explain a mismatch, but the correction must retain its source and history.

The [HHS individual right of access guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/access/index.html) underscores the importance of providing access to the appropriate designated record set. Practices should have privacy, legal, health information management, and system owners define how identity and record corrections are handled in their environment.

## Map the blast radius before disposition

The clinical record is only one dependency. Inventory scheduled and canceled appointments, inbound referrals, eligibility responses, claims, payments, statements, consent records, release requests, portal accounts, electronic prescribing interfaces, scanned documents, and external record exchanges as applicable.

For each object, capture its present record ID, date, source, transaction state, and whether movement is supported. Do not move an object merely because the two records will be linked. Some systems merge demographics but not balances. Others copy portal messages in one direction. Record those product behaviors in the runbook and test them outside production when possible.

Identify irreversibility. If a vendor cannot unmerge, the approval should acknowledge that constraint and require stronger pre-merge validation. Exporting a snapshot can aid reconstruction, but the practice must govern the export's access, storage, and disposal.

## Choose a precise disposition

Possible outcomes are broader than `merge` and `not duplicate`:

- confirmed duplicate, merge approved;
- same person, records remain separate pending dependency repair;
- different people with similar demographics;
- related or shared-contact records, not duplicates;
- insufficient evidence, monitor;
- system-created duplicate requiring vendor action;
- prior merge appears incorrect, correction review opened.

The authorized owner should record the outcome, supporting evidence classes, destination record if relevant, excluded objects, approval time, and execution owner. The investigation should never ask an administrator to infer a diagnosis, compare clinical narratives for identity, or decide disclosure rights.

If evidence remains insufficient, choose an observable next event and review date. `Pending` without a question or owner turns into permanent ambiguity.

## Execute in a controlled sequence

Before an approved merge, pause interfaces that could post new material during the operation when the system supports it. Confirm backups or vendor safeguards, capture pre-change identifiers, and notify only personnel who need to coordinate the change.

Run the documented operation under individual credentials. Record start and completion times and any system message. Then validate demographics, appointments, documents, messages, financial transactions, portal links, and interface queues against the dependency inventory. A technically successful merge dialog does not prove that every object landed correctly.

For a `not duplicate` decision, remove the temporary warning only after distinguishing fields or suppression rules are recorded. Otherwise, the same pair may be repeatedly flagged. Never alter a person's real data merely to stop a matching algorithm.

## Investigate an incorrect merge

Treat a suspected bad merge as a high-priority correction case under the practice procedure. Stop further redistribution, preserve audit logs, identify material added after the merge, and bring in the record, privacy, clinical, billing, and technical owners required by the incident.

Do not attempt a manual unmerge by deleting entries or rebuilding records from memory. Each item needs source attribution and a destination decision. Communications and disclosures already made may create additional review obligations that routine administrative staff should route, not decide.

## Close only after validation

A closure packet should contain the candidate IDs, trigger, comparison result, authorized disposition, dependency inventory, execution evidence, post-change checks, residual exceptions, and reviewer sign-off. Keep a cross-reference if the system and retention procedure require one, so later searches can explain why an old identifier no longer resolves normally.

Quality review should look for unsupported merges, investigations with no disposition, unresolved downstream objects, and repeated false-positive pairs. Report counts as administrative control findings. They do not demonstrate record accuracy in every respect or quality of care.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can two matching names and birth dates be merged automatically?

Not solely on that basis. Follow the practice's approved evidence threshold and authorized review process.

### Should one duplicate record be deleted?

Do not delete it as a shortcut. Use the system's governed merge, link, or correction process and preserve required history.

### What if transactions exist in both records?

Inventory them before disposition. Different transaction types may require different owners and may not move automatically during a technical merge.

### When is an investigation complete?

When an authorized disposition is recorded, required actions are validated, residual exceptions have owners, and temporary containment is appropriately removed or converted.

## Favor a defensible record over a quick merge

A duplicate investigation succeeds when another reviewer can see why the records were linked or kept separate and what happened to every dependent object.
