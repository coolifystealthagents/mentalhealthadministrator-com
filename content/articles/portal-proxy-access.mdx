## Set the portal proxy access scope

Control Portal Proxy Access starts with a written boundary. Name the client, the source, the owner, and the evidence. A clear rule keeps routine coordination separate from clinical judgment.

The main risk is that a proxy can receive access without the client consent or scope. Record the source and time before changing a status.

## Capture the evidence

A usable record includes client, proxy, relationship, consent, scope, approver, and status. Keep the source beside the record so another reviewer can reconstruct what happened.

The access lead confirms the decision or exception. The assistant can check consent, record scope, and route exceptions, but should not fill a gap with an assumption.

## Separate preparation from judgment

Decisions about privacy and access decisions remain with the authorized clinical or administrative owner. Use three labels: proceed, pause for missing evidence, and escalate.

## Run the review

Review missing consent, excessive access, and removal. For example, a family member requests portal access. Record what evidence was available and who owned the next step.

## Pilot one workflow

Start with one portal. Test the checklist on a small batch and review exceptions with the owner.
