A waitlist is not permission to contact someone through every available channel. A person may allow calls but no voicemail, permit text only for scheduling, use a shared household number, or request contact within limited hours. The preference record must be specific enough for the next worker to act without exposing why the person is on the list.

## Quick overview:

- Record permission and restrictions separately for phone, voicemail, text, email, and portal.
- Keep the waitlist reason out of messages unless an approved script requires a minimal reference.
- Define offer windows, attempt limits, and what happens after no response.
- Route clinical replies and requests for accommodation to authorized staff.
- Preserve preference changes with source and effective time.

## Design preferences as channel rules

A generic `okay to contact` checkbox is not sufficient. For each approved channel, capture `allowed`, `not allowed`, or `unknown`, plus source, recorded time, and any narrow instruction the system can enforce. Phone calls and voicemail should be separate. A permitted call does not necessarily permit a detailed message.

A practical structure may include:

| Field | Example |
| --- | --- |
| call permission | allowed |
| voicemail permission | callback number only |
| text permission | scheduling offers only |
| email permission | not allowed |
| portal permission | allowed |
| contact window | weekdays after 4 p.m. |
| language or communication request | routed reference, not diagnosis |
| attempt cap | value from approved waitlist policy |
| preference source | intake form, authenticated update, staff entry |

Use the person's exact operational request when it fits a controlled field. Do not put clinical history, household relationships, or the reason for seeking care in a free-text contact note.

The [HHS guidance on communicating with family and others](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/disclosures-to-family-and-friends/index.html) provides relevant privacy context. Practices should have authorized personnel determine how applicable privacy requirements and individual circumstances shape their contact procedures.

## Separate preference, consent, and message content

A preferred channel answers where to try contact. It does not by itself answer who may receive information, what may be disclosed, or whether a shared device is safe for detailed messages. Configure message templates independently from channel settings.

For example, a text may say that the office has an opening and provide a callback method without naming a service. A voicemail rule might allow only the practice name and callback number. The practice should approve each script and decide when identity must be verified before discussing an appointment.

If someone asks to use a representative, alternate number, or family contact, do not simply replace the primary details. Record the request, preserve current settings, and route any authority or disclosure question for review. Administrative convenience does not establish permission.

## Run an offer as a small, timed transaction

Create an offer ID linked to the waitlist entry and appointment slot. Record channel chosen under the preference, script version, sent or attempted time, delivery result, response deadline, and worker. The slot itself should have a clear hold rule so two staff members do not make incompatible promises.

Recommended states are `eligible for contact`, `offer prepared`, `offer sent`, `delivery failed`, `awaiting response`, `accepted pending booking`, `declined`, `expired`, and `exception review`. A delivery receipt is not acceptance. A callback request is not necessarily acceptance. Define the event that permits booking.

If several people may be contacted for one opening, the practice needs an approved sequence or simultaneous-offer rule. Staff should apply that rule consistently, not rank people based on message tone, diagnosis assumptions, or perceived urgency.

## Process preference changes at the right level

When a person says, "Do not text me again," record the channel change immediately under the approved identity procedure, stop pending text offers when technically possible, and note the effective time. Do not remove the person from every channel unless that was requested or the policy requires authorized review.

A request to leave the waitlist is different from a channel opt-out. Keep distinct closure reasons such as `appointment booked`, `person requested removal`, `attempt limit reached`, `unable to use approved channels`, and `transferred for review`. This distinction supports accurate follow-up without claiming why the person made the choice.

Preference history should be append-only from the worker's perspective. A correction can supersede an entry, but the audit trail should retain the previous value, source, author, and reason.

## Stop routine outreach when content changes the task

A reply may include symptoms, a safety concern, medication information, a complaint, or a request for clinical prioritization. The coordinator should not keep negotiating an appointment through the normal offer script. Preserve the message in the approved location and use the practice's urgent or clinical routing instructions.

Other stop conditions include an identity mismatch, a reply from an unexpected person, an accommodation request, a minor or proxy authority question, repeated delivery failure, or conflicting channel settings. The exception record needs the offer ID, relevant preference, received time, and receiving owner, not a broad copy to everyone on the scheduling team.

## Audit for respectful execution

Review whether the selected channel was allowed at the attempt time, whether the correct script version was used, whether the attempt occurred in the permitted window, and whether the stated deadline matched policy. Inspect expired offers for hidden late replies and check that opt-outs propagated to open tasks.

Useful process counts include attempts through disallowed channels, delivery failures, preference changes awaiting propagation, offers missing deadlines, and exceptions without owners. These measures do not show clinical access or treatment outcomes. Describe the sample and definitions when reporting them.

Test edge cases before launch: a shared mobile number, no voicemail permission, daylight-saving time changes, two simultaneous openings, a late acceptance after expiration, an accessibility request, and an urgent reply to an automated message.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a phone preference include permission to leave voicemail?

Not necessarily. Store and apply those permissions separately.

### Can a person stay on the waitlist after opting out of text messages?

Potentially, if another approved channel remains available and the practice's procedure allows it. A channel opt-out should not be treated as a universal removal without support.

### What should staff do with a clinical reply to an offer?

Stop routine scheduling communication and follow the practice's approved clinical or urgent routing procedure. Do not interpret or triage the content administratively.

### How much detail belongs in an opening message?

Only the content in the approved template for that channel and preference. Avoid diagnoses, treatment details, or an unnecessary explanation of the waitlist.

## Make the next contact predictable

A good preference record tells staff which channel may be used, what may be said, when an offer expires, and when the workflow must stop. It respects changes without turning scheduling data into clinical judgment.
