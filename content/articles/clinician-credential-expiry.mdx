## Set the clinician credentials scope

Track Clinician Credential Expiry starts with a written boundary. Name the client, the source, the owner, and the evidence. A clear rule keeps routine coordination separate from clinical judgment.

The main risk is that an expired credential can remain in the scheduling record. Record the source and time before changing a status.

## Capture the evidence

A usable record includes clinician, credential, expiry, source, reviewer, and next action. Keep the source beside the record so another reviewer can reconstruct what happened.

The credentialing lead confirms the decision or exception. The assistant can check the document, record expiry, and send reminders, but should not fill a gap with an assumption.

## Separate preparation from judgment

Decisions about credential approval and scheduling decisions remain with the authorized clinical or administrative owner. Use three labels: proceed, pause for missing evidence, and escalate.

## Run the review

Review expired credentials, missing documents, and review time. For example, a license expires during a busy intake week. Record what evidence was available and who owned the next step.

## Pilot one workflow

Start with one credential type. Test the checklist on a small batch and review exceptions with the owner.
