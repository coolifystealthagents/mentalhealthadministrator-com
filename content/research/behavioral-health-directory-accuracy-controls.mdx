Directory accuracy is not a single provider-level property. Name, phone, address, telehealth availability, accessibility information, languages, product participation, and referral availability can change independently. A control program must identify which field, location, product, and time period a claim describes.

This record studies the practice-side evidence chain. It does not certify payer directories, network adequacy, accessibility, language capability, or whether a provider is appropriate for an individual referral.

## Scope and control objective

The objective is to detect material differences between approved internal source records and specified directory destinations, route them, and retain correction evidence. The observation unit is a field at a provider-location-destination combination. Compound descriptions should be split when possible so one correct element does not mask another stale element.

Define active population rules as of a snapshot date. Include recently added, changed, and terminated relationships in dedicated strata. A directory entry absent from a destination is different from a present entry with a wrong value, and both differ from a destination that cannot be observed.

Use two complementary samples. A change-trigger sample follows recent internal changes to every relevant destination. A destination sample selects displayed entries and compares each field to its declared source. Preserve the query, capture time, destination view, and normalization rules.

> **Liftable statistic:** This editorial control model separates 4 stages: discrepancy detected, internal source approved, correction transmitted, and destination confirmed. Four is a proposed local control count, not a statutory timetable or performance benchmark.

## Source registry and three dates

For each field, name the internal authority, approving role, downstream destinations, and acceptable evidence. Keep source effective date, verification or capture date, and destination observation date separate. A value verified today can still have an older effective date, while a directory screenshot collected tomorrow only shows what was observable then.

Normalize cautiously. Formatting differences in phone numbers may be equivalent; suite numbers, location labels, and relay instructions may be material. Publish normalization rules before scoring. Do not treat fuzzy name matching as definitive without reviewing ambiguous cases.

## Preventive controls

At intake and change, require structured fields and applicable location relationships. Validate that a phone routes to the stated service point, but avoid sensitive test messages. Require an approved source before exporting changes. Generate destination-specific output from controlled data rather than retyping it into independent spreadsheets.

A preflight should detect missing identifiers, impossible effective periods, duplicate provider-location keys, required blank fields, and terminated locations still marked active. It should not decide whether a specialty description, accessibility statement, or panel status is substantively accurate without the authorized owner.

## Detective controls

Trigger reviews after provider departures, location moves, contact changes, product changes, and reported discrepancies. Periodic samples remain useful because unreported drift and failed interfaces may not create triggers. Oversample high-change records but also retain a random component.

Test the entire correction chain. Internal agreement alone does not show that a destination changed. Transmission evidence alone does not show that the destination applied the update. Store unconfirmed when confirmation is absent. If a public view varies by plan or search parameters, retain those parameters.

## Measures

Field agreement equals materially matching observed fields divided by comparable observed fields. Report inaccessible, absent, ambiguous, and not-applicable fields outside that denominator. Provider-location record accuracy can be reported separately as records with every critical field matching divided by comparable records.

Correction detection time runs from qualifying evidence received to exception creation. Internal correction time ends at approved-source update. Transmission time ends at outbound evidence. Confirmation time ends at a destination response or documented observation. These intervals identify different bottlenecks.

Also report repeat discrepancies, destinations with no confirmation mechanism, and false-positive exceptions caused by normalization. Counts should accompany rates, especially for small products or locations.

## Corrective action and governance

Assign each exception a field owner and destination owner. Preserve the conflicting values, sources, observation dates, submission reference, and next review. If a provider attestation conflicts with another approved source, route it under the field-specific precedence rule rather than selecting the convenient value.

Root-cause categories might include unreported internal change, export omission, destination rejection, delayed publication, ambiguous source, or matching failure. These are administrative categories, not blame findings. Retest the same field and destination after remediation.

## Limitations

Public directory views may be personalized, cached, product-specific, or inaccessible to automated review. Internal records can also be wrong. A sampled control gives an estimate for its frame and date, not perpetual accuracy. A correct directory field does not prove appointment availability, network coverage for a person, clinical fit, or effective communication. Requirements differ by program, jurisdiction, contract, and organization.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is provider attestation enough to mark a field accurate?

It is one source with a date and scope. Whether it is authoritative depends on the field and applicable process. Keep independent destination confirmation separate.

### Should unavailable destination fields count as errors?

Report them as unavailable unless the measure prespecifies absence as an error. Combining unavailable with incorrect hides an important control failure mode.

### How often should directories be checked?

No single cadence fits every field or destination. Use applicable requirements, contracts, change volume, risk, complaints, and prior error patterns to set documented frequencies.

### Does a confirmed correction prove referral availability?

Only if the confirmation specifically covers a defined availability field, conditions, and effective time. It does not establish clinical suitability or future capacity.

## Conclusion

Directory controls should follow fields through detection, approval, transmission, and confirmation. Change-trigger testing catches known events, while destination sampling detects silent drift. Separate dates, explicit unavailable states, and field-level denominators make the resulting evidence useful without overstating what a listing proves.
