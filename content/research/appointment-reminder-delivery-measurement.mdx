Reminder dashboards often compress several events into one reassuring label such as successful. That label can hide whether a reminder was merely queued, accepted by a communications vendor, delivered to a device, answered by a person, or acted upon by staff. This research record proposes a nonclinical method for measuring those events separately. It does not evaluate treatment, urgency, consent law, or whether a particular communication is legally permitted.

## Research question and unit of analysis

The central question is: for appointments eligible under a written reminder rule, what evidence exists that the planned communication moved through each administrative stage? The preferred unit is an appointment-reminder instance, not a patient and not a message alone. One appointment may create multiple attempts, while one batch transmission may contain many appointments.

Before extraction, declare the appointment types, locations, booking cutoff, channels, and reporting period. Exclude test records, appointments created after the reminder cutoff, and appointments canceled before the planned send time. Report each exclusion count. Do not silently remove records because the phone number was missing or a vendor response was unavailable. Those conditions are measurement findings.

The proposed local study joins a frozen appointment extract to raw message events by stable appointment and attempt identifiers. Reviewers retain the vendor's original event name, then map it to a versioned local vocabulary. A second reviewer checks a sample of mappings and all previously unseen event codes.

## Event model and evidence hierarchy

A useful event record contains appointment ID, attempt ID, planned channel, event code, event timestamp with time zone, source system, template version, and follow-up owner. The state sequence should permit branching. A message can be queued and then rejected, accepted and later marked undeliverable, or delivered without generating a reply.

Treat a vendor delivery event as evidence only of the event the vendor defines. It is not evidence that the intended person controlled the device, opened the message, understood it, or attended. A client reply is stronger evidence of interaction, but free text may require routing under practice policy. Staff should record the route without converting the reply into a clinical interpretation.

> **Liftable statistic:** This editorial protocol proposes 8 fields for each local reminder event record. Eight is a design count, not a published benchmark or a claim that every system requires the same fields.

## Measures and denominators

Use separate denominators because the questions differ:

* Transmission rate: attempts with a vendor acceptance event divided by eligible planned attempts.
* Delivery-evidence rate: accepted attempts with a qualifying delivery event divided by accepted attempts on channels that expose such evidence.
* Follow-up completion rate: triggered exceptions with documented disposition divided by exceptions whose response window ended during the period.

Show numerator and denominator beside each percentage. Also publish counts for missing contact data, suppressed communications, unmapped vendor codes, duplicate callbacks, and events arriving after extraction. For multichannel sequences, report both appointment-level reach and attempt-level delivery. Combining them can make extra attempts look like extra people reached.

Elapsed time should begin at a declared trigger, such as a permanent failure event, and end at a defined administrative disposition. Use system timestamps in one named time zone. If business-hour pauses are applied, describe the calendar and retain unadjusted duration too.

## Quality checks and failure modes

Reconcile a sample from scheduler to messaging platform and another sample in the reverse direction. The first detects planned reminders that never generated attempts. The second detects orphan messages, duplicate sends, or broken identifiers. Include at least one record from each channel and event family represented in the period.

Common failures include stale contact preferences, appointment changes after batch creation, copied template identifiers, vendor retries counted as new appointments, and webhook outages that make delivery unknown. Another failure is retroactively replacing a failure with the final successful state. Preserve both events so the operational burden remains visible.

## Privacy, accessibility, and role boundaries

Measure only information needed for the stated purpose. An analytic file usually does not need names, clinical narrative, diagnosis, or message-body text. Restrict access to linkage keys and raw replies. Practices should have authorized personnel determine applicable privacy, consent, language-access, accessibility, and communication rules.

Administrative measurement cannot decide whether a person needs urgent care, whether a channel is suitable, or whether nonresponse has clinical meaning. A predefined escalation route may carry the original communication to qualified staff, but the dashboard should describe the handoff, not claim resolution.

## Limitations

Vendor event semantics differ and may change without obvious notice. Device delivery behavior differs by carrier, operating system, email server, and user settings. Appointment outcomes also depend on scheduling lead time, transportation, health, preference, and many factors not represented in message logs. Missing events can reflect either true failure or failed telemetry. Comparisons across months are weak if channels, templates, eligibility rules, or vendor mappings changed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a sent reminder the same as a delivered reminder?

No. Sent should mean a specifically defined source event. Delivery requires a separate qualifying event and still does not establish that a person read or understood the content.

### How should messages with no vendor callback be classified?

Use unknown or callback missing, not failed or delivered. Record the expected callback window and investigate platform or integration logs.

### Should canceled appointments remain in the denominator?

It depends on cancellation timing and the prespecified measure. An appointment canceled before planned transmission may be excluded, while a cancellation after a reminder was sent remains relevant to transmission analysis.

### Can reminder metrics demonstrate improved attendance?

Not by themselves. An association can be influenced by appointment mix, lead time, staffing, season, channel selection, and other changes. A causal question needs a separate study design.

## Conclusion

Reliable reminder measurement begins with eligible appointment instances and preserves every technical event needed to reconstruct the path. Separate denominators, raw event retention, bidirectional reconciliation, and explicit unknown states reveal where communication evidence ends. The result is an auditable scheduling measure, not a claim about comprehension or clinical outcome.
