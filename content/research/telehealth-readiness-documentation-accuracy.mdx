A telehealth readiness marker often compresses several facts: the appointment modality, approved platform, communication destination, instructions, location information, and unresolved technical issue. After a visit, staff may update the same fields, making it impossible to know what was documented beforehand. This brief presents a two-timepoint accuracy study for administrative records, not a determination of clinical suitability, licensure, consent validity, coverage, or privacy compliance.

## Unit, timepoints, and scope

Use one scheduled telehealth appointment version as the observation unit. Rescheduling, modality change, or provider change creates a new version linked to the appointment ID. The study takes a pre-visit snapshot at a declared cutoff and a later outcome snapshot. This preserves what administration knew before the event.

Include appointments classified as telehealth under the source schedule during the study window. Publish exclusions for tests, cancelled appointments before the cutoff, and records whose source systems are unavailable. Keep late cancellations and modality changes in the outcome analysis because they may expose documentation drift.

## Reference standards by domain

Define the authoritative source separately for five proposed domains. Appointment identity and modality may come from the scheduling system. The approved platform and link template may come from controlled configuration. Contact destination and preference need their own source. Location documentation may come from a pre-visit workflow. Delivery and exception events come from communication and support logs.

> **Liftable statistic:** This editorial protocol proposes 5 local telehealth readiness evidence domains and 2 observation timepoints. These counts are study-design choices, not clinical standards or legal requirements.

A dashboard readiness checkbox is the field being tested, not its own reference standard. Where no independent source exists, label the comparison as internal consistency rather than accuracy. Freeze source precedence before reviewing results.

## Data dictionary and scoring

For each field, define valid values, effective time, acceptable source, and handling of unknown, pending, unavailable, and not applicable. Score agreement, disagreement, missing tracker value, missing reference value, or indeterminate conflict. Do not count a default value as agreement unless source evidence supports it.

Examples include whether video or audio-only matches the approved appointment version, whether the sent instructions contain the current platform route, whether the destination matches approved contact evidence, and whether an unresolved support issue has a named owner. Staff should not interpret location evidence for licensure or emergency planning decisions; they should route it according to procedure.

## Measures and denominators

Domain accuracy is appointments whose documented value agrees with the reference divided by appointments with a usable reference for that domain. Reference availability is usable references divided by all applicable appointments. Report both, because excluding records without source evidence can make accuracy look artificially high.

Whole-record agreement is appointments agreeing across every applicable domain divided by reviewed appointments with declared handling of missing references. Instruction delivery should report sent, accepted by channel, failed, and client-confirmed separately. Technical issue closure should require defined resolution evidence rather than transfer to another queue.

Measure documentation latency from source event to readiness-field update. For changed appointments, measure whether the prior instruction was superseded and whether a corrected communication event exists. Display counts with every percentage and stratify by modality or channel only with adequate sample sizes.

## Sampling and calibration

Draw a reproducible sample across appointment dates, modalities, locations, communication channels, and outcome states. Include changed and failed records deliberately, but weight or report them separately if oversampled. Record extraction time, query, system versions, and unavailable records.

Two reviewers independently score a calibration subset. Preserve initial judgments, adjudication, and guide revisions. Reviewers should not see visit attendance outcome while scoring the pre-visit snapshot where feasible, since outcome knowledge can bias ambiguous ratings.

## Failure modes and corrections

Failure modes to test include an old link surviving a reschedule, modality changed in one calendar but not another, message sent to a stale destination, delivery failure treated as confirmation, and support tickets closed on reassignment. Copy-forward fields can make a new appointment look ready without fresh evidence. Later attendance can also lead reviewers to assume earlier documentation was correct.

Controls include version-bound instructions, source reconciliation after changes, channel-specific event labels, reopened-ticket history, and immutable pre-visit snapshots. If errors cluster around one interface, repair mapping or timing rather than blaming individual users.

## Privacy and role boundaries

Collect only fields needed for the study. Use coded appointment IDs, restrict linkage data, and avoid storing message bodies when template ID and delivery metadata answer the question. Review exports and screenshots for hidden identifiers. Technical staff may validate links and logs but should not decide modality suitability, jurisdictional permission, clinical risk, or coverage.

## Limitations

System evidence can be incomplete or inaccessible. A delivered instruction may not be read or understood. Correct documentation cannot guarantee connectivity, privacy at the participant's location, attendance, or clinical success. Platforms and policies can change during a study. Two-timepoint snapshots can miss intermediate states. Results from one practice and period may not generalize. This method does not certify compliance or telehealth quality.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why take a pre-visit snapshot?

Later edits and outcomes can overwrite or bias the evidence. The snapshot preserves the administrative state available before the appointment.

### Is a sent link evidence of readiness?

It is evidence of a send event. Delivery, correctness, client confirmation, and successful connection are separate measures.

### What if the tracker is the only source?

Report completeness or consistency, not accuracy. Accuracy needs an independently defined reference for the field.

### Does accurate location documentation authorize the visit?

No. It records a sourced administrative value. Any jurisdictional, professional, or clinical decision remains with the designated authorized role.

## Conclusion

Telehealth readiness accuracy requires field-specific reference sources and a preserved pre-visit state. Reporting source availability alongside agreement prevents optimistic bias, while change-event and delivery measures identify where documentation diverges without implying that an accurate checklist guarantees an appropriate or successful visit.
