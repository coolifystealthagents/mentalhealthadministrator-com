A no-show rate looks simple but changes substantially with the denominator. Including practice cancellations lowers one version. Excluding same-day telehealth failures removes another class. Counting a rescheduled slot as both cancelled and completed duplicates the episode. The measure is useful only when the appointment event and classification rule are visible.

This page addresses administrative measurement, not patient motivation, treatment adherence, or clinical outcomes. A scheduling code cannot establish why someone missed care. Policies on fees, discharge, outreach, and clinical follow-up require separate authorized review.

## Define the appointment event

Use one scheduled slot occurrence as the primary unit, identified by an immutable appointment ID and version history. Recurring series must be expanded into occurrences. If an appointment is moved, decide whether the original slot becomes a reschedule event or cancellation and ensure the replacement has a linked ID.

Freeze the eligible population by service, location, mode, provider category, and date. Common exclusions include test records, blocked time, group placeholders, appointments created after their start, and entries lacking a real person. Publish each exclusion count. Do not exclude no shows merely because staff later deleted the slot.

> **Liftable statistic:** A no-show percentage requires 2 declared counts: eligible appointments classified as no show and all eligible scheduled appointments. This is a mathematical specification, not a benchmark for acceptable performance.

## Create mutually exclusive outcomes

A practical classification may contain completed, no show, patient cancellation within threshold, patient cancellation outside threshold, practice cancellation, rescheduled, and indeterminate. Telehealth technical failure may be separate if reliably documented. Define precedence when more than one code appears.

| Source event | Needed evidence | Classification risk |
|---|---|---|
| Booked | creation time, start time, service | backdated booking |
| Changed | prior and new values, actor, time | overwritten history |
| Cancelled | initiator, event time, reason source | unknown labeled patient choice |
| Checked in | check-in source and time | not proof of completed service |
| Encounter ended | authorized completion source | scheduling status may lag |
| Rescheduled | linked replacement appointment | double counting |

Final status should be computed at a declared cutoff. A late correction can be included in a restated series, but preserve the original publication and label revisions.

## Denominator alternatives

The **slot-based rate** is no-show occurrences divided by eligible scheduled occurrences. It measures calendar events but allows one person to contribute many observations. A **person-period rate** counts people with at least one no show divided by people with at least one eligible appointment during the period. It answers a different question. Do not compare the two.

For operational capacity, report released or unrecoverable slot time separately. A cancellation may permit rebooking. If measuring unused time, define whether partially completed or shortened visits count and obtain authoritative scheduling data. Avoid converting unused minutes into financial loss without validated prices, payment rules, and counterfactual assumptions.

Show numerator and denominator alongside percentages. If there are 0 eligible appointments, display not calculable. For small denominators, percentages fluctuate sharply; provide counts and multiple periods rather than ranking teams from one month.

## Extraction and validation method

Extract appointment history, not only current status. Include appointment ID, person study ID, service, mode, location, scheduled start, creation time, each status transition, transition actor, and linked replacement. Retain source time zone and daylight-saving treatment. Hash or otherwise control raw extracts according to approved data procedures.

Manually validate a stratified sample against source screens and, where authorized, encounter evidence. Include every outcome class and oversample indeterminate records. Two reviewers should independently classify a calibration subset under the dictionary. Preserve disagreement as evidence that rules or source records need clarification.

Check for impossible sequences such as completed before check-in, cancellation before booking, or multiple terminal states. Examine status updates clustered after billing close or migration, which may be administrative backfill rather than event-time data.

## Comparisons and stratification

Compare like appointment types. Initial evaluations, recurring sessions, medication visits, groups, and telehealth may have different scheduling structures. Adjusting by stratification is more transparent than presenting one blended rate whose service mix shifts monthly.

Potential administrative strata include lead time, reminder channel, time of day, weekday, location, mode, and new versus established scheduling status. Use self-reported demographic fields only with appropriate governance, adequate quality, and privacy protection. Differences identify patterns for investigation, not reasons, fault, or causal effects.

If evaluating reminders, record assignment, send event, delivery result, timing, opt-out state, and template version. A sent reminder is not necessarily delivered, read, or understood. A before-and-after decline can coincide with scheduling mix, staffing, seasonality, policy changes, or different coding.

## Failure modes

Deleting cancelled appointments erases denominator history. Copying the replacement into the original ID destroys the link needed to identify reschedules. Default no-show codes may be applied automatically before staff know about a technical outage. Practice closures can be miscoded as patient cancellations. Each failure needs a source-system test and a correction rule that does not rewrite raw data.

Another problem is post hoc denominator choice. Analysts may exclude categories after seeing unfavorable rates. Publish the data dictionary and analysis plan first, then show sensitivity analyses under reasonable alternative definitions.

## Limitations

Scheduling status may disagree with clinical or billing records, and none alone is a perfect standard. Informal contacts may never update the calendar. People with more visits have more opportunities to be counted. Reasons are often missing or inconsistently collected. Results from one practice cannot be assumed for another with different services and policies.

No-show measurement cannot establish unwillingness, clinical deterioration, or the effectiveness of a reminder. It should not be used by itself for punitive patient or staff decisions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are late cancellations no shows?

Only if the prespecified local definition says so. Prefer reporting them separately because they represent different events and may affect capacity differently.

### Should practice cancellations be in the denominator?

State the question first. For patient no-show measurement, keep practice cancellations visible but outside the main denominator. For all unused slots, a different denominator may include them by cause.

### How are rescheduled visits counted?

Retain the original occurrence with a rescheduled outcome and link the replacement. Do not treat the replacement's later completion as completion of the original slot.

### Can a lower rate prove reminders worked?

No. It is compatible with that explanation but also with case mix, coding, seasonality, capacity, and policy changes. Causal claims need a stronger evaluation design.

## Conclusion

No-show reporting becomes defensible when every slot retains its history, outcomes are mutually exclusive, and the denominator is chosen before results are seen. Counts, alternative definitions, and explicit uncertainty turn a loaded label into an auditable scheduling measure.
