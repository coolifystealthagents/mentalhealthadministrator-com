A referral handoff may involve intake notes, a consent artifact, an approved destination, selected documents, a transmission log, and a receiving response. If reviewers cannot reconstruct which permission supported which transmission, a “consent on file” checkbox offers little evidence. This brief presents a reproducibility study for administrative records. It does not determine legal validity, clinical appropriateness, or whether disclosure was permitted.

## Observation unit and research question

Use one outbound referral transmission as the observation unit. If a packet is sent twice, each transmission is an event linked to the same referral episode. This grain allows reviewers to evaluate the permission and destination effective at the actual send time.

The research question is whether two independent reviewers, using the same written guide, identify the same consent artifact, destination, scope, sender authority, transmission event, and receipt state. Agreement reveals whether records are interpretable. It does not prove that an agreed interpretation is legally correct.

## Building the review packet

For each sampled transmission, assemble references to the referral episode, consent version, destination identity, approved scope, sender or approving role, transmission record, and receipt evidence. Keep source materials unchanged. Mask direct identifiers in reviewer worksheets where feasible and retain a separate linkage file.

The six evidence components scored are consent artifact, destination, permitted content scope, authorized sender or approver, transmission, and receipt state. Each receives supported, unsupported, not applicable, or indeterminate. Indeterminate covers inaccessible, conflicting, or ambiguous evidence and should not be scored as passing.

> **Liftable statistic:** This editorial protocol proposes 2 independent reviewers and 6 handoff evidence components for a local reproducibility test. These counts are study-design choices, not legal requirements or proof of valid consent.

## Reviewer guide and calibration

Define what qualifies as a versioned artifact, how names and organizations are matched, which timestamp governs, and how amended or withdrawn permission is handled. The guide should prohibit inferring scope from diagnosis, referral reason, prior relationships, or usual practice. Reviewers should have access only to evidence needed for scoring.

Both reviewers independently score a calibration subset before discussion. Report component-level raw agreement and retain the two original ratings. A chance-adjusted statistic may be added when categories and sample size support it, with its method stated. If disagreement clusters around one rule, revise the guide and repeat calibration on new records.

## Measures for completeness, agreement, and timing

Packet completeness is transmissions with all applicable components supported divided by eligible transmissions reviewed. Reviewer agreement uses all independently rated component decisions as its denominator. Do not combine these measures: two reviewers may agree that a packet is incomplete.

Transmission interval begins at the authorized send-ready event and ends at system transmission. Receipt interval ends at destination acknowledgment under a declared rule. A fax success signal, secure-message delivery state, and human acknowledgment are different evidence. Label the observed state rather than calling all three received.

Report counts for unavailable artifacts, obsolete versions, destination mismatches, scope ambiguity, missing approvals, failed sends, and unknown receipt. Stratify by channel only when samples are adequate. Publish excluded tests, cancelled events, duplicates, and inaccessible legacy records.

## Chain-of-custody tests

Compare the document manifest with approved scope without requiring reviewers to read clinical content beyond what is necessary. Verify destination identifiers against the authorized record, especially where a directory entry changed. Preserve resends and corrections. A corrected transmission should not erase the failed or misdirected event.

Test whether the consent was effective at send time and whether a later amendment was improperly back-applied. Confirm that transmission logs belong to the sampled event rather than another referral. If a potential inappropriate disclosure appears, stop ordinary scoring and use the designated privacy process.

## Failure modes and remediation

Defects to test for include a checkbox without an artifact link, scanned forms with no version or effective date, destinations recorded only in free text, document lists created after sending, and receipt inferred from absence of an error. Another risk is reusing prior consent for a new destination without an authorized determination.

Remedies differ: immutable links for provenance, controlled destination IDs, generated manifests, channel-specific receipt labels, or an exception queue for scope conflicts. Training alone will not repair a system that discards send details. Remediation should preserve role boundaries rather than instruct administrators to interpret ambiguous permission.

## Limitations

The study measures reproducibility from recorded evidence. Reviewers can consistently apply an incorrect guide. Missing records may reflect migration or channel limitations rather than absent action. Rare destinations and channels may not appear in a sample. Applicable consent and disclosure requirements vary. Receipt evidence does not prove that the receiver used or understood the information. The method does not certify compliance or referral quality.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why use the transmission rather than the referral as the unit?

Permission, scope, destination, and documents can change between sends. Transmission-level review preserves the state effective for each event.

### Does reviewer agreement prove valid consent?

No. It shows that the written evidence and guide produce consistent classifications. Authorized personnel must determine legal and policy validity.

### What if the destination never acknowledges receipt?

Record the strongest available channel evidence and leave human receipt unknown. Do not convert a successful send signal into acknowledgment.

### Should withdrawn permission overwrite the earlier record?

No. Preserve the prior version, withdrawal evidence, effective time, and actions taken after it.

## Conclusion

Reproducible handoff records connect each transmission to the exact permission, destination, scope, approval, manifest, and channel evidence in effect at that time. Independent review distinguishes ambiguous records from consistent ones while avoiding the unsupported claim that completeness or agreement establishes lawful disclosure.
