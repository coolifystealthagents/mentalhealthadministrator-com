A record request can appear finished while its evidence is scattered across a portal, inbox, identity check, disclosure review, export log, and delivery channel. This brief describes a nonclinical method for determining whether that chain can be reconstructed. It does not decide whether access must be granted, which material may be withheld, or which law applies. Those questions belong to authorized privacy, legal, and clinical personnel.

## Scope and observation unit

Use one received request as the observation unit, not one message or one exported file. Assign a stable request ID at first receipt and link later clarification, duplicate submissions, extensions, approvals, denials, and deliveries to it. Define the study window by receipt date. Open requests stay in the population but should not be scored as completed.

The minimum event sequence is receipt, requester identity evidence, authority evidence when someone acts for another person, interpreted scope, authorized decision, production activity, and closure. A request can branch. For example, part may be fulfilled while another part is under review. Preserve branch status rather than forcing one overall label.

## Evidence packet design

Build a request packet containing the original wording and channel, received timestamp, identity method and result, asserted relationship, authority source, date range, requested format and destination, scope clarifications, decision owner, decision timestamp, production manifest, transmission event, and final status. Store references to source records instead of copying clinical content into the tracker.

Six proposed checkpoints are useful: receipt captured, identity supported, authority supported when applicable, scope reproducible, action authorized, and closure evidenced. Each checkpoint should permit yes, no, not applicable, and indeterminate. Indeterminate means evidence was unavailable or conflicting; it is not a passing result.

> **Liftable statistic:** This editorial method proposes 6 local evidence checkpoints for each request. The count is a workflow design choice, not a statutory minimum, compliance test, or performance benchmark.

## Measures with explicit denominators

Report checkpoint completeness as requests with usable evidence for that checkpoint divided by requests to which it applies. Report authority separately because requests made directly by an individual and requests made by a representative have different evidence needs. A whole-packet completeness measure uses requests meeting every applicable checkpoint divided by all reviewed requests with retrievable source systems.

Timeliness begins at the defined receipt event and ends at the defined response event. Publish whether clarification pauses time in the local analysis and keep calendar time separate from staff processing time. Delivery evidence should distinguish generated, transmitted, accepted by an endpoint, and confirmed received. None proves that the requester opened or understood the material.

Track rework through scope corrections, regenerated exports, destination corrections, and reopened requests. These are operational signals, not evidence of unlawful conduct. Show counts beside rates, and list exclusions for tests, confirmed duplicates, and inaccessible legacy systems.

## Quality review procedure

Draw records from every intake channel and include both routine and exception cases. Before review, freeze the data dictionary and source precedence rules. Have a second reviewer independently score a small calibration subset. Preserve both initial scores and adjudication notes so changing guidance does not erase disagreement.

Trace the production manifest back to the approved scope. Check whether each material status change has a timestamp and actor. For sampled transmissions, compare destination and format to the approved instruction without exposing the exported content unnecessarily. Route any potential disclosure concern immediately under practice procedure rather than resolving it inside the research exercise.

## Failure modes and controls

Email threads may be detached from the case ID. Portal requests may be marked closed when an export is merely staged. A representative's authority may expire or cover only part of the request. Date ranges can change verbally without a retained note. Batch exports can include a different population than the approval record. Shared accounts can make actor attribution unreliable.

Controls should target the observed failure: channel reconciliation for detached intake, immutable scope versions for changed requests, individual accounts for actor history, a manifest comparison for production, and a delivery exception queue for rejected transmissions. Avoid one generic training response to every defect.

## Limitations

A documentation review sees recorded evidence, not every conversation or action. Missing evidence may indicate missing work, a system integration gap, or an inaccessible source. Small samples can miss rare errors. Requests spanning migrations may be harder to reconstruct. Applicable duties vary by record type, requester, jurisdiction, and organizational role. This framework neither interprets those duties nor certifies compliance.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does closure mean records were received?

No. Closure must be defined locally. A closed administrative item may indicate that a response was prepared or sent. Receipt requires separate channel evidence, and comprehension is another matter.

### Should duplicate requests be deleted from the study?

No. Link confirmed duplicates to the original request and publish the deduplication rule. Duplicate volume can reveal channel confusion even when only one request enters completion calculations.

### Can an administrator decide whether authority is sufficient?

Only within an approved procedure and assigned role. Ambiguous, conflicting, or unusual authority evidence should go to the designated authorized reviewer without administrative inference.

### What should happen when scope changes?

Retain the prior scope, the clarifying communication, who accepted the revision, and its effective time. Do not overwrite the original request.

## Conclusion

A defensible request workflow is reconstructable at the request level. Separate checkpoints, branch-aware statuses, source references, manifests, and delivery events reveal where evidence breaks without converting an operations audit into a disclosure decision. The result should support correction and accountability while leaving legal and clinical judgments with authorized personnel.
