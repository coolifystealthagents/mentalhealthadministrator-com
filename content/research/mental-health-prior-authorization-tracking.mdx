Prior authorization tracking fails when a practice stores one status but the payer process contains many events. A request can be prepared, transmitted, rejected at intake, accepted, paused for information, clinically reviewed, decided, appealed, or superseded. The tracker should preserve those events and their sources rather than replacing them with pending.

This is administrative research guidance. It does not determine medical necessity, coding, coverage, urgency, appeal strategy, or final patient responsibility. Payer, product, jurisdiction, service, and effective date matter. Qualified personnel must interpret the applicable rule and provide clinical content.

## Identify the authorization object

Use one payer request for one defined service scope as the unit. Store plan and product, member identifier in the approved system, provider and location identifiers, service or code set as supplied by authorized staff, requested date range or units, request type, payer case number, and source-system link.

Do not assume a benefits inquiry is an authorization. Do not interpret no authorization required as a guarantee of payment. Preserve the exact payer response, timestamp, channel, and reference. If a request is resubmitted, link versions without erasing the rejected or withdrawn event.

## Event ledger and evidence precedence

A robust ledger may use eight administrative events: requirement checked, packet ready, transmitted, payer receipt acknowledged, additional information requested, response transmitted, decision received, and reconciliation closed. Each event should identify actor, source, timestamp, and supporting reference.

| Status | Evidence | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Requirement checked | dated payer source and product | not a coverage guarantee |
| Ready | required administrative items present | clinical sufficiency decided elsewhere |
| Submitted | transport confirmation | not payer receipt |
| Received | payer acknowledgment or case ID | not approval |
| Information due | exact payer request and due statement | staff do not invent clinical facts |
| Response transmitted | authorized response content and transport evidence | not payer acceptance or decision |
| Decision | payer notice and effective scope | authorization is not payment |
| Closed | reconciliation result and owner | preserve later changes |

When portal, telephone, fax, and electronic transaction responses conflict, retain all sources and route the discrepancy. A telephone reference should include called number, date, representative or reference if supplied, and exact statement without embellishment.

> **Liftable statistic:** CMS finalized 72-hour expedited and 7-calendar-day standard decision time frames for certain prior authorization requests to impacted payers, generally beginning in 2026. These time frames are scoped, not universal deadlines for every payer, plan, service, or incomplete request.

## Rule register before clock calculation

Create a versioned rule register by payer, product, request class, service category, jurisdiction, and effective dates. Cite the authoritative source and record who approved the local interpretation. Distinguish calendar hours, calendar days, business days, and any conditions affecting when a request is considered complete.

The CMS final rule is relevant to defined impacted payers and provisions. A tracker must not apply its headline time frames to every commercial plan or request. Likewise, a portal display can change. Save the applicable notice or source reference according to policy.

Calculate elapsed time only after defining start, stop, and pause events. Show raw elapsed calendar time as well as rule-adjusted time when useful. Never overwrite receipt time to match the date a case number appeared. Unknown applicability should stay unknown and receive an owner.

## Audit methodology

Construct the population from both the practice submission log and payer acknowledgments. Searching only decided cases omits failed transmissions and open requests. Reconcile by case number plus member, service, and date fields, protecting identifiers in analytic files.

For each sampled request, trace requirement evidence, authorized order or service scope, packet preparation, transmission, receipt, information requests, responses, and final notice. Sample across payers, service categories, channels, and outcomes. Include unresolved and rejected-at-intake records.

A second reviewer should examine deadline exceptions and source conflicts. Clinical reviewers should assess clinical-content questions; administrative reviewers can test whether the approved content was included and transmitted. Record unavailable source documents as unavailable, not as timely or late.

## Operational measures

* **Receipt confirmation rate:** submissions with payer receipt evidence divided by transmissions reviewed.
* **Intake rejection rate:** requests rejected before substantive review divided by acknowledged attempts, grouped by exact reason.
* **Information-response traceability:** payer information requests with a linked authorized response event divided by information requests received.
* **Decision aging:** open acknowledged requests in elapsed-time bands under the applicable rule register.
* **Decision turnaround:** time from defined start to decision for decided requests, stratified by payer and request class.
* **Reference integrity:** tracker case numbers resolving to the matching payer record divided by case numbers tested.
* **Unreconciled decision count:** payer notices received but not matched to the practice request.

Publish counts with percentages and distinguish open requests from missing outcomes. Decided-only turnaround suffers survivorship bias because the longest cases may still be open.

## Exception and escalation design

Examples include member mismatch, wrong payer endpoint, expired order, missing portal access, contradictory payer instructions, duplicate case number, and request for clinical information. Give each exception a named administrative owner and deadline source. Route medical-necessity narratives, peer review, coding changes, and expedited classification to authorized professionals.

Test fallback procedures during portal outages. Preserve when the outage was observed, alternative channel used, and payer response. An internal escalation documents that the issue moved; it does not stop a payer clock unless the applicable source says so.

## Limitations

Payer systems can revise statuses without complete histories. Telephone statements may be difficult to verify. Product identification can be wrong, and requirements can change during a course of service. Decisions observed by the practice may arrive later than the payer's internal decision time. Local samples cannot support national payer comparisons.

An authorization does not prove claim payment, and a denial code does not by itself reveal whether the clinical decision was correct. Administrative timeliness cannot establish health impact. Results should be used to repair handoffs and evidence, not to fabricate certainty about coverage.

## Frequently asked questions

### When does the clock start?

Use the start event in the verified rule applicable to that payer, product, request, and date. Submission, receipt, and completeness may be different moments.

### Is a portal screenshot enough evidence?

It can evidence what the portal displayed at a time if captured under policy, but may not contain the full notice or history. Keep source, timestamp, case number, and later changes.

### Can staff fill in missing clinical rationale?

No. They can identify the payer request and route it to the authorized clinical owner. They should not create, alter, or infer clinical facts.

### Does prior authorization guarantee reimbursement?

No. Authorization and payment are separate processes subject to claim and coverage details. Preserve the payer's exact qualification.

## Conclusion

A useful authorization tracker is an event ledger anchored to payer-specific sources. It distinguishes transmission from receipt, open from missing, and authorization from payment. Versioned rules and authorized ownership let practices monitor work without turning administrative status into a coverage promise.
