A waitlist is both a set of current records and a history of access attempts. Counting names today answers a different question from following everyone added last month. This brief provides an administrative measurement design for entries, offers, contacts, appointments, removals, and unresolved records. It does not assess urgency, treatment fit, diagnosis, or level of care.

## Define the unit and populations

Use one person-request episode as the observation unit. A new episode begins under a written rule, such as a request for a materially different service after the earlier episode closed. Keep duplicate contacts linked to the episode rather than counting each message as new demand.

Maintain two populations. The census population contains episodes active at a specified instant. The entry cohort contains episodes added during a period and is followed to stated outcomes. Census measures describe current workload but overrepresent long waits. Cohort measures support elapsed-time analysis but remain incomplete until enough follow-up has occurred.

Eligibility requires a documented administrative entry rule. Publish whether records awaiting professional review, records missing requested information, internal transfers, and people who deferred an offer remain active. Never use an administrative list position as a substitute for a qualified priority decision.

## Event model and three clocks

Record entry, readiness for scheduling under the approved process, each offer, each outreach attempt, response, booking, first attended appointment when available, deferral, transfer, and removal. Every event needs a timestamp, source, actor or automated process, and reason code where applicable. Corrections should preserve the replaced value.

Use three separate clocks. Calendar age runs from entry to the reporting instant or closure. Ready-to-offer time starts when the administrative prerequisites defined by the practice are met. Offer-to-book time runs from a specific offer to acceptance or expiry. These clocks expose different bottlenecks and should not be added together without a declared episode rule.

> **Liftable statistic:** This editorial model proposes 3 local waitlist clocks: calendar age, ready-to-offer time, and offer-to-book time. Three is a measurement design choice, not a clinical standard or promised access interval.

## Measures that retain their denominators

Entry volume is eligible new episodes during the period. Active census is eligible open episodes at the snapshot. Offer rate is episodes receiving at least one qualifying offer divided by cohort episodes eligible for offers during follow-up. Booking rate can use offers or episodes as its denominator, but the label must say which.

Report time distributions with medians, ranges or percentiles, and counts still open. A mean alone can hide a long tail. For an immature cohort, state the follow-up cutoff and show unresolved episodes. Do not calculate completed-case wait time and imply that it represents people still waiting.

Removal reasons should include booked, person declined further contact, unable to reach under the approved protocol, transferred, no longer eligible under an authorized decision, duplicate, and unknown. Show unknown separately. Administrative staff should record an attributed reason, not infer why a person did not respond.

## Offer and outreach evidence

An offer should identify a real slot, service, modality, location, and expiry under the local definition. General statements that appointments may become available are outreach, not offers. Sent, delivered, answered, and accepted are distinct states. If a communication platform lacks delivery evidence, mark it unavailable.

Accessibility and communication preferences affect what an attempt means. Record approved channel, accommodation or language-routing status where relevant, and the source of those instructions. Keep message content minimal in analytic extracts. An outreach count cannot establish that the person understood the message.

## Sampling and reconciliation

Reconcile the list against appointment events, referral status, and communication logs. Sample recent entries, oldest open records, removed records, and episodes with many attempts. Stratify by service or location only when categories are stable and cell sizes support responsible reporting.

For each sample, verify entry evidence, current status, latest contact, offer details, removal authority, and correction history. A second reviewer should independently score ambiguous reason codes before definitions are revised. Keep the original extract and query so later results can be reproduced.

## Failure modes and interpretation

Common defects include resetting entry dates after transfer, deleting declined offers, counting mass messages as appointment offers, closing records when a message is sent, and excluding old records from reports. Merged identities and reopened episodes can also distort age. Address these with event history, controlled offer criteria, explicit closure evidence, and linkage rules.

A falling census can reflect more appointments, fewer entries, removals, data cleanup, or changed eligibility. A longer median can occur when old records are finally resolved. Report inflow, outflow by reason, and definition changes together. Administrative results do not measure treatment quality or population need.

## Limitations

Waitlist systems may omit requests received elsewhere. Communication logs may not prove delivery. Capacity and demand change seasonally. Different services may not be comparable. Small cohorts yield unstable estimates, while long follow-up delays reporting. Reasons supplied by people or referrers may be missing and should not be guessed. This framework offers no universal target and does not certify access compliance.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which count should appear on a dashboard?

Use both a dated active census and period entry volume. Label them clearly because one is a snapshot and the other is a flow.

### Does accepting an offer end wait time?

Only under a declared definition. Booking, attendance, and service initiation are different events and may warrant separate measures.

### How should unreachable records be handled?

Follow the approved contact protocol, retain attempt evidence, and use an attributed closure reason. Do not equate no response with lack of need.

### Can age determine who receives the next slot?

Not by itself. Slot assignment and any clinical priority judgment must follow the authorized practice process.

## Conclusion

Useful waitlist reporting combines census and cohort views, preserves offers and removals as events, and keeps three clocks separate. Transparent eligibility, unresolved counts, and attributed closure reasons allow administrators to identify process friction without turning queue age into a clinical decision or a promise of access.
