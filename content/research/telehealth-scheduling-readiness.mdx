Scheduling readiness is the ability to show that the current appointment version has the approved administrative ingredients for a telehealth attempt. It is not a prediction that the visit will occur or a decision that telehealth is clinically appropriate, covered, or permitted. This brief focuses on appointment creation, change control, instructions, communication events, and exception ownership.

## Define the appointment version

Use one appointment version as the working unit. A change in date, time, provider, service, location, or modality should create a linked change event. Readiness inherited from an earlier version must be re-evaluated under a written rule. Otherwise, a rescheduled video visit may retain an obsolete link or instruction.

Eligible appointments are those marked for telehealth at a defined pre-visit cutoff. Publish whether same-day additions, group events, audio-only appointments, and appointments cancelled before cutoff are included. Test records and confirmed duplicates should be counted as exclusions.

## Six-checkpoint workflow

The proposed local workflow checks six items: current appointment identity, approved modality, approved platform route, contact and communication instruction, required administrative status, and exception owner. Each checkpoint should reference its authoritative source and effective time. Required administrative status might include a locally approved consent-record state, but staff should not infer legal validity from a checkbox.

> **Liftable statistic:** This editorial workflow proposes 6 local pre-visit checkpoints for telehealth scheduling readiness. Six is a design count, not a federal requirement, clinical standard, or promise that a visit will connect.

Use ready, not ready, pending, not applicable, and source unavailable as controlled states. Ready should require all applicable checkpoints under the current appointment version. A pending professional decision cannot be changed to ready merely to clear the scheduling queue.

## Modality and location handling

Video, audio-only, and in-person are distinct scheduling values. A fallback from video to audio-only should not be assumed. Record an approved change and issue the appropriate updated instruction. Administrative workers may relay approved choices but should route questions about clinical suitability, jurisdiction, prescribing, privacy, and coverage.

Location fields should capture what the approved workflow requires and identify the source. They should not invite an administrator to decide whether a clinician may proceed. If location information is missing or changes, send the exception to the named owner and preserve the original value and update.

## Communication states

Separate message sent, channel-delivered, and person-confirmed. A system send timestamp proves only the first state. A delivery receipt may support the second. A reply or approved confirmation event supports the third. None proves comprehension. Document channel, destination reference, template version, language or accessibility routing where applicable, timestamp, and result.

When an appointment materially changes, test whether old instructions were superseded and new instructions were issued. Avoid exposing sensitive detail in message content. Failed or bounced messages need an assigned follow-up state, not automatic completion after another send attempt.

## Measures and denominators

Readiness rate is appointment versions meeting every applicable checkpoint at the cutoff divided by eligible versions reviewed. Checkpoint completion uses eligible appointment-checkpoint pairs as the denominator. Report both because a single recurring defect can depress whole-appointment readiness.

Change reconciliation is materially changed versions with required updated instructions divided by versions requiring an update. Communication delivery rate is channel-delivered messages divided by sent messages with an observable delivery outcome. Report outcome availability separately as sent messages with an observable outcome divided by all sent messages, so missing telemetry cannot inflate the delivery result. Confirmation rate uses eligible appointments under the local confirmation protocol. Never substitute sent messages as the denominator for all appointments without explaining why.

Exception age runs from detection to authorized resolution or cutoff. Show unresolved counts and age bands. Stratify by modality, channel, or location only when definitions are stable and small groups can be reported responsibly.

## Operational review

At a defined lead time, query upcoming telehealth appointment versions. Reconcile each against the provider calendar, modality approval source, platform configuration, communication log, and exception queue. Repeat a narrower change check closer to the appointment when the workflow supports it.

Sample ready and not-ready records, reschedules, modality changes, bounced communications, same-day bookings, and platform outages. Verify source references rather than relying on the consolidated checklist. Keep extraction time, query, and scheduling-system version so the review can be reproduced.

## Failure modes and controls

Copy-forward can preserve a former provider's link. A calendar move may not trigger a new message. Staff may interpret a sent reminder as confirmation. Group appointment links can be attached to the wrong event. A support issue can be marked closed when transferred. System outages may create mass exceptions that are later deleted.

Version-bound links, material-change triggers, channel-specific statuses, distinct transfer and resolution states, and retained outage annotations address these mechanisms. Review role access to link templates and exported schedules. Do not ask staff to improvise unapproved platforms or modalities to improve readiness rates.

## Limitations

Readiness documentation cannot guarantee equipment, connectivity, privacy at a remote location, attendance, or a successful clinical encounter. Communication evidence may be incomplete. Local system integrations and policies vary. Same-day changes can occur after the cutoff. Payer and jurisdiction rules can change and need qualified review. Outcome comparisons may be confounded by modality, population, scheduling lead time, and service type. This framework does not certify compliance.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is audio-only a backup whenever video fails?

Not automatically. Treat it as a distinct modality and follow the approved professional, legal, payer, and practice process.

### When should readiness be measured?

Choose and publish a cutoff tied to an operational action. If a second check is used, report both snapshots rather than replacing the earlier result.

### Does delivered mean the person understood the instructions?

No. Delivery is a channel event. Confirmation and comprehension require different evidence and should not be inferred.

### What happens after a reschedule?

Create or identify the new appointment version, re-evaluate affected checkpoints, supersede obsolete instructions, and retain the change history.

## Conclusion

Telehealth scheduling readiness depends on version-aware checks, controlled modality values, source-linked instructions, and explicit exception ownership. Separating sent, delivered, and confirmed communication prevents overstatement. The workflow helps administration prepare an approved appointment while reserving clinical, legal, coverage, and jurisdiction decisions for authorized roles.
