Referral intake converts information from a person, referrer, portal, fax, or phone call into a packet for the right authorized review. A single required-field percentage can be misleading because routes have different requirements and populated fields may be inaccurate. This brief defines administrative completeness while preventing an intake checklist from becoming clinical screening.

## Scope and observation unit

Use one referral episode as the unit. Link all messages, documents, duplicate submissions, and corrections under that episode. Define when a materially new request creates a new episode. The eligible population should state channels, locations, service lines, receipt dates, and whether self-referrals and internal referrals are included.

Completeness means the applicable administrative evidence is present, not applicable under the route dictionary, or unavailable from its named source after the declared procedure. Not supplied and requested and pending remain incomplete. Completeness does not mean the person is suitable for a service, that urgency has been assessed, that coverage is confirmed, or that every supplied fact is correct.

## Route-specific data dictionary

Create requirements by route rather than one maximal checklist. A self-referral may have a different source and permission path from an external professional referral. For every field, define applicability, accepted format, source, whether verification is required, owner, and consequence when absent.

Five proposed domains are identity and contact, communication instructions, requested administrative service, supplied source documents, and named review destination. Within each domain, retain four null states: not supplied, requested and pending, unavailable from source after the declared procedure, and not applicable under the route dictionary. Only the latter two can be permitted completeness values when the written rule applies. Blank should not silently mean any of these.

> **Liftable statistic:** This editorial model proposes 5 local administrative domains and 4 null states for referral completeness review. These counts are workflow choices, not a validated intake instrument, federal requirement, or clinical screening standard.

## Separate capture from verification

Record who supplied a fact and when. A phone number entered from a fax is captured, not necessarily verified. A directory lookup may verify an organization but not the sender's authority. Keep supplied value, verification source, verification result, and verification time distinct.

Corrections should append history. If two dates of birth or destinations conflict, mark a conflict and route it under procedure. Do not choose the value that appears most plausible. Administrative workers can request clarification using approved language but should not manufacture clinical facts to complete a packet.

## Measures and calculations

Field completeness is applicable fields with an accepted value, a documented not-applicable state, or a source-unavailable state that met the declared attempt rule, divided by all applicable fields reviewed. Not supplied and requested and pending stay outside the numerator. Episode completeness is episodes satisfying every required field for their route divided by eligible episodes. Because one missing field can fail the episode measure, publish domain-level results too.

First-review readiness measures packets meeting administrative requirements when first presented to the authorized reviewer. Rework counts episodes returned for missing or conflicting administrative information. Request-to-response interval starts at the missing-information request and ends when the specific item arrives or the episode closes. Report unresolved requests at cutoff.

Show numerator, denominator, exclusions, and route mix. A higher completion rate after excluding fax referrals is not comparable with an earlier all-channel rate. Avoid combining “not applicable” with missing, and do not report unavailable source records as verified.

## Handoff design

A handoff record needs episode ID, receiving role or queue, reason, packet version, timestamp, sender, acceptance or acknowledgment state, and unresolved items. Transfer is not review completion. If the destination rejects or redirects the packet, retain that event and the reason supplied.

Clinical descriptions received from a referrer should be preserved in the approved source location and routed without administrative reinterpretation. The intake tracker can reference the document and status instead of copying detailed narrative. Urgent language should follow the practice's designated escalation procedure; the measurement project must not create its own triage rule.

## Quality sampling

Sample across channels, routes, referrer types, and packet outcomes. Include packets returned for rework, closed before review, and still pending. Freeze the route matrix and reporting cutoff. Compare tracker fields with source messages and documents, then verify that the recorded receiving queue matches the handoff event.

Use a second reviewer for fields with ambiguous applicability. Preserve original scores during adjudication. If a route routinely creates an exception, revise the workflow or dictionary rather than simply marking the exception complete.

## Failure modes

Duplicate faxes may become two episodes. A portal can require a value even when it is not applicable, encouraging placeholders. Staff may mark packets complete after sending a clarification request. Attachments can arrive without linkage to the episode. A queue may accept the packet technically while no authorized reviewer owns it.

Controls include duplicate-link review, route-specific forms, explicit pending states, attachment reconciliation, and acknowledged ownership. Monitor placeholders and default values because they can inflate completeness. Do not use higher completion as evidence that referrals were clinically appropriate or ultimately scheduled.

## Limitations

Source documents may contain errors that completeness review cannot detect. Channels may have unequal retrievability. Requirements can vary by service, payer, jurisdiction, and organization. Sampling may underrepresent abandoned or unlinked referrals. Communication accessibility cannot be inferred from a preferred-language field alone. Changes in route mix can alter rates without any process improvement. This methodology does not certify access, privacy, or clinical quality.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is every field required for every referral?

No. Define applicability by route and purpose. Collecting unnecessary information increases burden and privacy exposure.

### Does complete mean ready to schedule?

Not necessarily. It means the declared administrative packet is ready for its designated next review. Professional decisions may remain outstanding.

### How should contradictory information be scored?

Mark it conflicting, retain both sources, and route it. A populated field with unresolved conflict should not count as verified.

### Is a handoff complete when staff send the packet?

Only if the chosen measure is transmission. Ownership or review requires separate receiving evidence and status definitions.

## Conclusion

Referral completeness is credible when it is route-specific, source-attributed, and explicit about null states. Domain measures, first-review readiness, and handoff evidence reveal where intake stalls while preserving the crucial boundary between assembling information and deciding clinical suitability.
