An EHR access review should answer three different questions: who can authenticate, what each identity can do, and whether that capability still has documented authorization. A staff roster alone cannot answer them. Neither can an account export, because generic, service, dormant, vendor, emergency, and test identities may not appear in human resources data.

This page offers an administrative evidence method. It is not a security certification or legal conclusion. The organization must determine which HIPAA provisions and other requirements apply, and qualified security and privacy personnel must decide access appropriateness and incident response.

## Establish authoritative populations

Use separate source populations and reconcile them. The workforce source identifies current workers, start and end dates, status, and supervisor. The identity source identifies accounts and authentication status. The authorization source contains approved role or privilege evidence. Vendor and service identities need their own accountable sponsor and purpose.

Assign a stable review ID that does not expose unnecessary personnel information. Preserve extraction time and system version. Never merge solely on display name: name changes and reused names create false matches. Preferred keys may include an immutable workforce ID and the EHR identity ID, linked through a controlled mapping.

Classify unmatched records rather than deleting them. An enabled EHR identity with no workforce match may be a legitimate contractor, integration, stale account, or mapping defect. A current worker without an identity may not need EHR access. Each state requires investigation, not an automatic conclusion.

## Review capability, not labels

Role names such as therapist, front desk, or administrator are not evidence of their underlying permissions. Export effective privileges, including inherited group access, location scope, record categories, break-glass functions, report exports, prescribing or ordering features if present, and user-management powers. Compare that effective capability to the approved access profile.

| Evidence object | Essential fields | Review concern |
|---|---|---|
| Human identity | unique ID, status, affiliation | orphan or duplicate account |
| Approval | approver, scope, date, basis | stale or missing authorization |
| Effective access | role, groups, locations, functions | privilege accumulation |
| Authentication | method, last success, lock state | inactivity is not proof of misuse |
| Change ticket | request, implementer, completion time | request differs from provisioned result |
| Service identity | owner, function, credential controls | shared accountability |

> **Liftable statistic:** HIPAA technical safeguards include unique user identification for covered systems subject to the rule. This requirement supports attribution, but a unique ID alone does not prove least privilege, appropriate activity, or compliance.

## Reproducible review method

Freeze extracts from the workforce, EHR, identity provider, vendor register, and access-request system at a documented cutoff. Normalize dates and identifiers in a working copy while retaining raw exports read-only. Produce exception classes through version-controlled matching rules.

For a full census, review every enabled account and every privileged role. If a large organization samples ordinary privileges, publish sampling strata and still census high-impact and unmatched identities. Validate a subset against source screens because exports may omit inherited permissions or represent configuration labels inaccurately.

The reviewer should not approve their own access. Record reviewer, decision, evidence, and date. Where a supervisor attests to a role, make clear whether they reviewed a role label or the actual privilege list. Unsupported access should enter a controlled remediation queue with an interim decision by authorized security leadership.

## Measures that expose uncertainty

* **Roster match rate:** enabled human identities matched to an active approved affiliation divided by enabled human identities. Show unmatched contractors separately.
* **Authorization coverage:** reviewed identities with current evidence supporting effective privileges divided by identities whose privileges were reviewable.
* **Privilege mismatch count:** identities with at least one effective permission outside the approved profile. Count identities and permissions because one identity may have many differences.
* **Deprovisioning interval:** elapsed time from authoritative termination or role-change event to verified access change. Describe negative or missing intervals rather than correcting them silently.
* **Unowned service identities:** enabled nonhuman identities without a current accountable sponsor.
* **Review evidence completeness:** decisions containing reviewer, source snapshot, result, and timestamp divided by completed review decisions.

A low last-login count is a lead for review, not proof that an account is unnecessary. Conversely, recent use does not prove the access is authorized. Activity monitoring and access authorization are related but distinct controls.

## Joiner, mover, leaver tests

Simulate a new hire with delayed start, a worker moving locations, a contractor expiration, immediate termination, leave of absence, and return from leave. Check when the source event becomes visible, who authorizes the change, whether inherited groups change, and how completion is verified in the EHR rather than merely in a ticket.

Mover events are often the hardest because new access is added while old access remains. Compare pre-change and post-change effective privileges. For urgent removal, document the authoritative trigger and escalation route. Do not invent a universal deadline; applicable policy and risk determine timing.

## Logging and exception handling

Audit logs require governance. Define which events are collected, retention, clock synchronization, access to logs, alert ownership, and review procedure. A log existing is not the same as anyone reviewing it. NIST guidance can inform log management, while the organization's risk analysis should shape implementation.

Potential unauthorized activity belongs with the designated privacy or security process. An access reviewer should preserve evidence and route it, not announce a breach or infer motive. Keep investigation status separate from access-remediation status so disabling an account does not falsely imply the investigation is complete.

## Limitations

Exports can be stale, APIs can omit local configuration, and identity mappings can fail. Some privileges depend on context that static files do not capture. Reviewers may approve familiar names without examining details. Vendor identities and emergency accounts may operate outside routine workflows. The method cannot show that every access was appropriate merely because permissions matched an approved role.

Small practices may have unavoidable role overlap. Document independence limitations and compensating review rather than claiming segregation that does not exist. Regulatory applicability, documentation periods, and required safeguards need qualified assessment.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an annual review always sufficient?

No universal cadence is asserted here. Reviews should also respond to starts, role changes, departures, system changes, and identified risk. Leadership should set and document the applicable schedule.

### Should disabled accounts remain in the review?

They can remain in reconciliation data with a disabled state and evidence. Removing them can obscure deprovisioning history or identity reuse. Retention should follow approved policy.

### Are shared accounts acceptable?

Unique attribution is an important safeguard. Any technical exception should be evaluated by authorized security and compliance personnel, with ownership and compensating controls documented rather than assumed.

### Does matching the access matrix prove appropriate access?

It proves only consistency with that matrix at the snapshot. The matrix itself may be outdated, and observed use may still require separate review.

## Conclusion

Effective EHR review reconciles people, identities, approvals, and actual privileges. It makes unmatched and nonhuman accounts visible, validates implementation after role changes, and routes activity concerns without premature conclusions. The deliverable is a traceable decision set, not a compliance badge.
