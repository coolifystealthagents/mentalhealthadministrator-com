# Clinical Boundary Matrix

## Purpose

Mental Health Administrator offers remote administrative support to mental health practices. Virtual assistants perform nonclinical administrative work only. This matrix separates repeatable administrative actions from decisions that must remain with licensed clinicians, authorized practice leaders, payers, legal counsel, or emergency resources.

This document is an operating boundary, not legal advice, a compliance certification, a clinical protocol, or proof that any engagement meets a particular law or standard.

## Universal exclusions

Virtual assistants do not provide crisis response, suicide or violence risk assessment, diagnosis, treatment, triage, clinical screening, counseling, medication guidance, level-of-care decisions, coding judgment, legal advice, or compliance certification. They do not claim to be clinicians or emergency responders. A client must define the qualified owners and emergency pathways before work begins.

## Responsibility matrix

| Workflow | Assistant may perform | Must remain with qualified or authorized owner | Stop and route trigger | Administrative evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Intake and referral | Log inquiries, collect approved demographic fields, send approved forms, track receipt, organize a packet | Clinical fit, urgency, risk, diagnosis, level of care, acceptance, provider selection based on clinical need | Symptoms, safety language, urgent request, uncertain suitability, missing authority | Intake checklist, source timestamp, document status, reviewer handoff |
| Appointment scheduling | Offer approved slots, book approved visit types, send templates, process routine changes, maintain an administrative waitlist | Clinical prioritization, timing based on symptoms, visit-type conversion, care frequency, emergency access decisions | Request for clinical advice, deteriorating condition, safety concern, scheduling outside approved rules | Calendar entry, message history, exception queue, schedule handoff |
| Client communication | Answer approved office-process questions, send forms or directions, log attempts, route messages | Clinical interpretation, counseling, medication advice, treatment direction, complaint adjudication | Medication, symptom, safety, abuse, self-harm, harm to others, or clinical-content message | Original message, approved reply, transfer time, receiving owner |
| Insurance administration | Submit eligibility inquiries, record payer responses, track authorization status, request administrative documents | Interpretation of coverage, clinical criteria, medical necessity, service choice, financial advice | Conflicting payer response, denial rationale, clinical information request, coverage dispute | Dated payer response, reference number, status tracker, owner handoff |
| Billing and claims follow-up | Submit approved claims, check status, record responses, post from approved remittance rules, organize corrections | Diagnosis and procedure coding, modifier choice, appeal argument, balance decision, refund, write-off, collection action | Code or charge change, clinical documentation request, disputed balance, appeal decision | Submission receipt, payer status, remittance record, authorized decision |
| EHR and records administration | Index documents, update permitted demographic fields, log requests, flag possible duplicates | Clinical authorship, note amendment, identity resolution, profile merge, disclosure approval, retention policy | Uncertain identity, amendment request, release ambiguity, access question, possible duplicate | Document index, request log, access record, authorized resolution |
| Provider credentialing | Gather source documents, enter approved facts, track applications, log payer requests, schedule reminders | Provider signature, attestation, professional disclosure, eligibility decision, legal or regulatory interpretation | Attestation, sanctions question, unsupported fact, discrepancy, request to sign for provider | Source checklist, provider approval, submission receipt, payer correspondence |
| Reporting and workflows | Define approved administrative measures, reconcile counts, maintain queues, document procedures | Clinical outcome conclusions, care-quality judgment, provider ranking, regulatory conclusion, management decision | Request to infer care quality, score a clinician, profile a client, or certify compliance | Metric definition, source report, reconciliation record, approved procedure version |

## Message handling rule

1. Preserve the client or caller's words when routing a protected topic.
2. Do not ask follow-up questions intended to evaluate symptoms, danger, diagnosis, or care need.
3. Use only the practice-approved transfer instruction and emergency notice.
4. Record the time, channel, designated recipient, and transfer status.
5. Do not document that a person is safe, stable, low risk, cleared, or appropriate for routine follow-up.

## Authority rule

A written client instruction can authorize an administrative action, but it cannot turn a nonclinical assistant into a clinician, coder, attorney, compliance officer, emergency responder, or authorized signer. If an instruction crosses that line, work pauses and the item returns to the client's responsible owner.

## Required setup before service

The client must provide named owners for clinical messages, safety-related messages, scheduling exceptions, coding questions, records releases, access approvals, payer disputes, and credentialing attestations. The client must also provide current templates, system permissions, business hours, after-hours directions, and stopping rules. If these are absent, the affected workflow does not begin.
