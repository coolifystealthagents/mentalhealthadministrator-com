# Service Opportunity Assessment

## Decision standard

A service earns a dedicated record only when it addresses a distinct buyer problem, can be performed remotely as nonclinical administrative work, produces identifiable deliverables, supports a complete four-step workflow, and can be described without promising clinical, payer, legal, compliance, or financial outcomes.

The catalog is intentionally limited to eight services. Page count is not evidence of value. Closely related tasks stay together when separating them would create thin or repetitive offers.

## Separation rationale

Intake coordination and scheduling are separate because intake ends at qualified review and administrative routing, while scheduling governs an approved calendar after placement rules exist. Client communication is separate because it controls channels, templates, transfers, and unresolved ownership across workflows.

Insurance administration is separated from claims follow-up because benefit and authorization source responses occur before or during access administration, while claims work begins with authorized billing inputs and payer transaction status. Neither service includes coding judgment.

Records administration is separate from credentialing because client records and provider professional files have different owners, authority, and disclosure risks. Reporting and workflows uses approved operational data across the practice but does not replace any source workflow or produce clinical conclusions.

## Rejected or consolidated opportunities

| Candidate | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Crisis line or crisis response | Reject | Requires immediate safety response and qualified protocols beyond nonclinical administration. |
| Clinical intake screening | Reject | Screening, risk evaluation, diagnosis, and level-of-care decisions require qualified clinical judgment. |
| Therapy, coaching, or treatment planning | Reject | Direct clinical service is outside scope. |
| Medication management support | Reject as a service | Medication advice and clinical monitoring are outside scope. Routine messages may only be routed. |
| Behavioral health coding | Reject | Code selection, modifier choice, and coding validation involve judgment not delegated here. |
| Compliance audit or certification | Reject | The service does not provide legal advice, attest compliance, or certify controls. |
| Records release decision service | Reject | Administrative tracking is included in records support, but disclosure authority remains with the client. |
| Denial appeals service | Consolidate narrowly | Deadline and status tracking fit claims follow-up. Appeal strategy and clinical rationale do not. |
| Referral matching service | Consolidate narrowly | Nonclinical directory fields fit intake coordination. Clinical suitability and provider selection do not. |
| Separate reminder service | Consolidate | Approved reminders are part of scheduling and communication and do not justify a distinct service. |
| Data entry service | Consolidate | Data entry is a task within each controlled workflow, not a standalone mental health offer. |
| HIPAA consulting or BAA service | Reject | No HIPAA compliance, BAA availability, legal, or certification claim is made. |

## Risk controls required for all eight services

Every engagement requires a written task scope, least-necessary data definition, approved systems and channels, individual access where supported, named clinical and administrative owners, exception and stopping rules, current templates, retention instructions, and access termination steps. Clinical content and urgent or safety-related language are routed without assistant assessment.

Outputs must preserve source and status. A payer response is not a coverage guarantee. A submitted claim is not a payment promise. A credentialing submission is not enrollment. A dashboard is not a clinical conclusion. An organized intake packet is not acceptance or a care recommendation.

## Catalog conclusion

All eight selected records meet the opportunity standard and have distinct buyers, problems, tasks, deliverables, processes, scenarios, and boundaries. Additional pages should be added only after documenting a new buyer problem, nonclinical remote feasibility, distinct outputs, and a boundary model that does not duplicate these records.
